## Q1. Competition facts: official rules, dates, deliverables, and participant-visible updates

### Takeaway
The Devpost page has the authoritative rules: 4 tracks, 5 unweighted criteria, and $1,500/$1,000/$500 prizes. The deadline is Oct 25, 2026 at 22:00 GMT+8, which is 14:00 UTC. Several official sources contradict each other on timing, video length, and scoring, and nobody had answered the open forum questions when I checked. The IEEE group's own hackathon page lists weighted criteria that put explicit weight on **data security** and a **climate-impact / carbon-reduction projection**. Those weights show what the organizers care about, even if Devpost is the page judges actually use.

### Cited Findings
**Basic facts (Devpost overview)**

Tags: Blockchain, Machine Learning/AI, Social Good. Host label: "IEEE Turkey". — [Devpost overview](https://ieee-climatechain-hack.devpost.com/)

**Tracks and the official "Challenge → Problem → Build" table (Devpost)** — [Devpost overview](https://ieee-climatechain-hack.devpost.com/)

Suggested builds: blockchain carbon-credit verification, real-time emissions tracking, smart contracts to prevent double counting, transparent carbon trading platforms.
2. **Renewable Energy & Energy Trading.** Problem: "Energy markets remain centralized and inefficient despite renewable growth". Suggested builds: P2P energy trading, renewable certificate tracking, microgrid coordination, tokenized incentives for clean energy.
3. **Sustainable Supply Chains.** Problem: "Difficult to verify ethical sourcing and sustainability claims". Suggested builds: product lifecycle tracking, **Digital Product Passports**, recycling traceability, circular-economy incentive platforms.
4. **Climate Data & Environmental Monitoring.** Problem: "Environmental data is fragmented and difficult to verify". Suggested builds: climate-data verification platforms, IoT environmental monitoring, climate-data marketplaces, research and insight tools.

**Judging criteria (Devpost, no weights given)** — [Devpost overview](https://ieee-climatechain-hack.devpost.com/); [Devpost rules](https://ieee-climatechain-hack.devpost.com/rules)

- Innovation & Creativity: asks for a "unique approach to applying blockchain or emerging technologies… within the chosen track".
- Technical Execution: asks whether the project "function[s] as intended" and makes "effective use of tools such as blockchain, smart contracts, AI, data platforms, or environmental data integration".
- Practical Usefulness: asks whether "organizations, communities, policymakers, or businesses" could realistically use it.
- Presentation & Communication (called "Presentation & Storytelling" on the rules page): the demo must convey (1) the climate problem, (2) how the solution works, and (3) the potential real-world environmental impact.

### Inferences
- [Inferred] Treat **Oct 25, 2026, 14:00 UTC (22:00 Taipei time)** as the hard deadline and submit at least several hours early. Devpost enforces the sidebar time, and the "8 AM PT" sentence on the rules page looks like a leftover template error. The organizers have not clarified it.
- [Inferred] Devpost has no track selector, so the track has to be stated explicitly in the title or tagline and at the top of the description. The required file upload (≤35 MB, per a participant's report) is a natural place for a PDF covering the IEEE page's "hidden" deliverables: architecture diagram, security and data-integrity assessment, climate-impact and carbon-reduction projection, and roadmap. Doing this covers both rubrics.
- [Inferred] It is still unconfirmed whether blockchain is mandatory. The title, the sponsor (IEEE Blockchain), and every judge's affiliation point to blockchain as expected in practice. A project without a real on-chain component, even in Track 4, would likely do poorly on "Innovation… applying blockchain" and "Technical Execution… smart contracts".
- [Inferred] Every training session and update kept returning to the same words: trust, data integrity, security, privacy, responsible AI, and measurable impact. The organizers want **"AI + blockchain + trustworthy data" with a security story**, not a token-speculation app.
- [Inferred] With 804 registrants and prizes of only $3,000, the IEEE Senior Member nomination and the COP31 showcase may be the real draw for academic teams.

### Takeaway
A small Turkish IEEE volunteer workgroup (IEEE Blockchain Türkiye, chaired from Gazi University) runs the hackathon with Orange County and Silicon Valley IEEE blockchain and EMBS volunteers. This is the group's **first hackathon**. Before this it ran summits and workshops.

### Inferences
- [Inferred] Because this is a first-time event, the rules are messy (typos, conflicting deadlines and weights), and there are no past winners to model. Judges will probably score largely on gut feel against the 5 Devpost criteria. A polished, clearly structured submission that addresses every criterion by name will stand out.
- [Inferred] The Istanbul Chamber of Commerce (İTO) represents Istanbul businesses, many of them EU exporters. A solution relevant to Turkish industry, such as CBAM or ETS compliance, or DPPs for exporters, may appeal to that stakeholder. This is speculative because no İTO-specific challenge is published.

### Inferences (what the panel will likely reward)
- [Inferred] **Security and integrity carry real weight.** Four judges (Sağıroğlu, Akleylek, Seyhan, Iqbal) are security or cryptography researchers, and the IEEE page gives data security 10% and requires a "security and data integrity assessment". A one-page threat model (sensor spoofing, oracle manipulation, Sybil identities, double counting, key compromise) with concrete mitigations will score well. Examples: signed sensor data, Merkle proofs, DIDs or verifiable credentials, ZK proofs, and a note on post-quantum-ready signatures. Mentioning crypto-agility or PQC (e.g., NIST ML-DSA/Kyber) is a cheap differentiator that Akleylek and Seyhan will notice.
- [Inferred] **Fidan, the team leader and probably the most influential judge, builds DPPs and runs a climate-tech incubator.** A Track 3 Digital Product Passport project, or carbon-footprint tokenization or RWA for green energy, matches his business focus directly. He will also likely value startup-style adoption plans and EU-regulation fit (ESPR, CBAM).
- [Inferred] **Track 2 has two domain experts** (Soran on P2P and VPP DeFi trading and DSO blockchain; Oyucu on battery and renewable forecasting AI). A naive P2P-trading demo will be judged against Soran's own published work, so it needs grid-realistic details: DSO role, settlement, forecasting with AI. Battery-related ideas, such as a battery passport combined with AI state-of-health, would appeal to both Oyucu and Fidan.
- [Inferred] **Track 4 and Track 1 judges care about provenance.** Iqbal (digital twins plus blockchain supply-chain threat modeling) and Duman (Hyperledger Fabric plus IoT cold chain) lean toward IoT-to-ledger traceability. Permissioned chains such as Hyperledger Fabric are familiar and acceptable to this panel; a public-chain token is not required.
- [Inferred] **Healthcare-and-climate crossovers** (heat or air quality and health, cold-chain vaccines) could resonate with the EMBS/healthcare cohort (Datta, Kodgi, Airre, Duman) and with COP31's health theme. This is a niche angle, not a requirement.

## Q4. COP31 context and Türkiye-specific climate policy

### Takeaway
COP31 runs **Nov 9–20, 2026 in Antalya** (the EXPO 2016 site renamed the "COP 31 Area"). Türkiye holds the presidency and leads the Action Agenda; Australia's Chris Bowen is "President of Negotiations". Türkiye frames it as an **"Implementation COP"** with themes of zero waste and methane, electrification, oceans, food security, resilient cities, green industrialization, and clean energy. At home, Türkiye's first **Climate Law (No. 7552, July 2025)** created a national ETS with a **2026–2027 pilot covering CBAM sectors**, as **EU CBAM** begins charging Turkish exporters of steel, cement, aluminium, fertiliser, electricity, and hydrogen from 2026. ## Q6. What wins comparable blockchain/AI-for-climate hackathons

### Takeaway
Recent winners share a pattern: **real-world data in (IoT, sensors, satellite, or operational data) → verifiable on-chain record or certificate (often NFT or registry based, aligned with a recognized methodology) → simple end-user verification (dashboard or QR code)**, with **AI** for verification or prediction, **anti-greenwashing**, and **double counting** as the selling points. Pure token or DeFi plays rarely win sustainability tracks.

### Cited Findings
- **Hedera Hello Future Origins (Jul 21 – Aug 8, 2025; $150k pool), Sustainability track** — [Hedera blog](https://hedera.com/blog/these-are-the-winners-of-the-hello-future-origins-hackathon/)
  - 1st ($15k) **GreenTrace**: GHG emissions tracking and verification with NFT certificates, an offset marketplace, and **GRI-compliant reporting**.
  - 2nd ($10k) **Nodebility**: AI "Bionodes" plus Hedera Guardian turn organic waste into biogas energy and **verifiable carbon credits**, with tokenized BIOGAS and DAO governance. This is a waste-to-energy and methane theme.
  - 3rd ($5k) **Veritas**: records product sustainability claims on Hedera Consensus Service, and consumers **verify via QR code**. This is an anti-greenwashing, DPP-like project.
- **Hedera Hello Future Hackathon 2.0 (Nov–Dec 2024)**: 1st overall ($35k) went to **Ecosphere Prime**, a "decentralized hyperlocal climate intelligence oracle" in which IoT sensor climate nodes produce data validated on Hedera. Another winner **digitized the Verra VM0048 REDD+ methodology** on Hedera Guardian; sources disagree on its placing. — [Hedera blog (2.0 winners)](https://hedera.com/blog/these-are-the-winners-of-the-hellofuture-hackathon-2-0/) (summary via search; the page returned a 503 when I tried to fetch it directly)
- **Hedera Hello Future Ascension**: 1st in Sustainability ($10k) was **ChargeFrog**, a carbon-negative EV-charging network with a Guardian-based **dMRV** that auto-mints and retires NFTs to verify offsets "in near real time" and reduce greenwashing. — [Hedera blog (Ascension)](https://hedera.com/blog/these-are-the-winners-of-the-hello-future-ascension-hackathon/) (via search summary)
- Gold Standard and ATEC issued the first fully digital cookstove credits using **dMRV traceable on Hedera Guardian** (Mar 2026), showing that dMRV is the current industry direction. — [Gold Standard](https://goldstandard.org/news/first-fully-digital-cookstove-carbon-credits-issued-publicly-traceable-on-hedera-guardian)
- **ETHPrague 2023** sustainability winner: Plantation Station, a decentralized agent service that monitors and optimizes crop yield. — [Devfolio recap](https://devfolio.co/blog/unleashing-innovation-in-the-heart-of-europe-a-recap-of-ethprague/)
- **Gitcoin Climate Solutions round (GG19, Nov 2023)**: about $150k matching pool and 127 projects. Eligible categories included **"Oracles & dMRV"**, renewable energy, carbon accounting, and nature-based solutions. Example grantees: Treegens (AI-verified mangrove planting, soulbound NFTs), RealClime (real-time climate data market). — [Chaine Research GG19](https://chaineresearch.substack.com/p/gg19-quadratic-funding-result); [Gitcoin checker](https://checker.gitcoin.co/public/round/JGWl9gGbW/show)
- **Build With Celo × Toucan (2023)**: a tokenized-carbon dApp hackathon with a $20k pool; winners were not listed in the extracted text. — [HackerEarth BWC Series 1](https://bwcseries1.hackerearth.com/)
- **Open Earth Foundation**: ran the Open Climate Collabathon (2019, Yale OpenLab; 350 participants, 11 projects; prompts included nested climate accounting) and the COP27 Global Stocktake Climate Datathon (Hewlett $100k). It also built a Chainlink CO₂ concentration oracle. No winner lists were found. — [Hewlett grant](https://hewlett.org/?p=29921539); [Chainlink blog](https://blog.chain.link/open-earth-receives-grant-for-c02-oracle)
- Other hackathon-style projects (not verified winners): Mandla Rice MRV, a Stellar Soroban plus GPT-based MRV for smallholder rice that claims on-chain records prevent double counting. — [lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/ai-hackathons/co-creating-with-gpt-5/saturns/mandla-mrv-blockchain-system)

### Inferences
- [Inferred] Winning recipe adapted to this panel:
  1. **A specific, credible problem** tied to a named framework or regulation, such as CBAM, TR-ETS, EU DPP or battery passport, Verra or Gold Standard methodologies, GRI, ICVCM, IEEE 3218, or COP31 Zero Waste.
  2. **A real data pipeline** (public datasets, satellite, or a simulated IoT device with signed payloads) rather than hard-coded numbers.
  3. **AI that does a defensible job**: anomaly or fraud detection, forecasting, document extraction, or uncertainty estimation, with explainability, which matters to Oyucu, Sağıroğlu, and Airre.
  4. **A blockchain that does something only a ledger can do**: unique issuance and retirement to prevent double counting, a tamper-evident audit trail, verifiable credentials, cross-party settlement.
  5. **A working testnet demo with a clean UI** and a QR or verification flow.
  6. **A quantified climate impact estimate** (tCO₂e avoided, kWh, kg waste diverted) with stated assumptions.
  7. **A security and threat-model section.** This is the distinctive extra for this IEEE panel.
- [Inferred] Very crowded ideas include generic "carbon credit marketplace" and "P2P solar trading" demos. With 804 registrants, many teams will build these. Differentiation can come from narrowing to a niche with a regulation hook. Examples: CBAM embedded-emissions passport for Turkish steel or aluminium SMEs; a battery passport with an AI state-of-health oracle; waste-methane dMRV for landfills tied to Zero Waste; PQC-ready signed IoT sensor attestations for climate data.
