# Track 4: Climate Data & Environmental Monitoring — Current State, Pain Points, Existing Solutions, Unmet Needs

Research date: 2026-10-14. Notes are in English with Chinese terms where useful. Facts older than 2024 are flagged [OLDER]. Most findings come from search-result snippets of the cited pages; several primary pages (UNEP press releases, the UNEP methane data portal) returned HTTP 403 and could not be fetched, so the figures attributed to them come from secondary coverage, as noted.

## 1. Emissions monitoring gaps: reported vs. actual emissions, methane super-emitters, BTRs

### Takeaway
Official self-reported inventories still undercount emissions by large margins, especially methane from energy. Satellites now detect super-emitters routinely, but **the bottleneck has shifted from detection to response and accountability**: about 88–90% of UN methane alerts still get no response, and the US (the second-largest alert recipient) answered none. Independent monitoring capacity is also fragile: MethaneSAT was lost in 2025, and US carbon satellites (OCO-2/3) were proposed for termination.

### Cited Findings
**Inventory vs. reality**
- [OLDER, 2021] A Washington Post investigation of 196 country reports to the UN put the gap between reported emissions and what reaches the atmosphere at **8.5 to 13.3 billion tonnes/yr**. The high end is close to China's annual emissions and about 23% of humanity's warming contribution. Main causes: land-sink subtractions (e.g., Malaysia subtracted more than 243 Mt CO2 for forest absorption in its 2016 inventory), undercounted methane, and underreported fluorinated gases — [The Hill summary](https://digital-release.thehill.com/policy/energy-environment/580504-massive-gap-found-between-nations-reporting-emissions-analysis/amp/); [Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/us/2021-11-08/countries-climate-pledges-flawed-data-united-nations-3540046.html); [Planetizen](https://planetizen.com/node/115246)
- IEA Global Methane Tracker 2025: energy-sector methane runs **above 120 Mt/yr**, and total energy methane is about **80% higher than what countries report to the UNFCCC**. Few countries use measurement-based data (Europe is the main exception). Cloud cover (Venezuela) and snow and ice (northern Russia) still leave satellite blind spots. Abandoned wells and mines added about 8 Mt in 2024. The 80% figure is via secondary summaries — [IEA news](https://www.iea.org/news/methane-data-and-transparency-continue-to-improve-but-emissions-remain-far-too-high); [ADDA247 summary](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/key-findings-from-global-methane-tracker-2025/); [AirQualityNews](https://airqualitynews.com/headlines/methane-emissions-much-higher-than-they-need-to-be-says-iea/)
- [OLDER, 2022–23] Climate TRACE: oil and gas production emissions among top UNFCCC-reporting countries were **up to 3 times higher than reported**, roughly 1 Gt above inventory figures — [Climate TRACE news](https://climatetrace.org/news/climate-trace-releases-first-comprehensive-independent); [Al Gore summary](https://algore.com/news/this-al-gore-backed-project-found-that-we-re-seriously-undercounting-greenhouse-gas-emissions)
- Climate TRACE full-year 2025 data: global GHG emissions **reached a record 60.63 Gt CO2e (+0.50%)**, methane rose 1.03% (+4.2 Mt CH4), and oil and gas production was the fastest-growing subsector (+4.1%). Russia had the largest country increase — [Hellenic Shipping News, reprinting the Climate TRACE release](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/?p=1124552)
- Climate TRACE scale: more than 660 million facilities or sources (Nov 2024 inventory). Later 2025 dashboards show about **2.77 million emissions sources summarized from about 744.7 million assets** (v5.x). Climate TRACE says revisions are "common and expected" — [Climate TRACE news](https://climatetrace.org/news/more-than-70000-of-the-highest-emitting-greenhouse-gas); [Zenodo inventory](https://zenodo.org/records/18379672); [Climate TRACE Oct 2025 data](https://climatetrace.org/news/climate-trace-releases-october-2025-emissions-data)

**Methane alert and response (UNEP IMEO MARS)**
- UNEP *An Eye on Methane* (5th edition, ~Oct 2025): government and company responses to MARS alerts rose **from 1% to 12%** in a year, and nearly 90% remain unanswered. More than **3,500 alerts** have gone to 33 countries. A later UNEP release (Bloomberg Philanthropies expansion) gives 13% — [IISD SDG Knowledge Hub](https://sdg.iisd.org/news/un-report-calls-for-scaling-action-on-methane-through-data-driven-solutions/); [UNEP press release](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/press-release/better-data-driving-action-methane-emissions-more-work-needed); [Eco-Business reprint](https://www.eco-business.com/press-releases/unep-partners-ramp-up-efforts-to-address-major-methane-leaks-as-un-chief-urges-rapid-cuts/)
- IMEO now publishes **country-level response rates**: the share of alerted sources that got a response, over a rolling 12 months with a 2-month lag (page dated ~May 2026) — [UNEP methane data, MARS snapshot](https://methanedata.unep.org/story-MARS-snapshot)
- An IEA–IMEO guidance document on responding to MARS notifications says engagement "remains low" even though it has risen since MARS launched in 2023 — [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/responding-to-satellite-notifications-from-the-methane-alert-and-response-system)
- Late Aug 2026: **Turkmenistan received 192 MARS alerts in a year** and responded to about 20%. It accounted for 3 of the 6 largest leaks worldwide in the previous six months. **The US received 138 alerts, responded to none, and fixed no leaks.** Turkmenistan has now stopped 8 leaks (corroded pipes, faulty wells, failing flares) — [Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/world/arid-41904757.html); [National Herald](https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/environment/turkmenistan-begins-fixing-methane-mega-leaks-after-years-of-scrutiny-criticism)
- Carbon Mapper 2025 persistent-emitter analysis: 15 of the top persistent sites, including the top two, are in Turkmenistan's Balkan region. Esenguly plumes averaged 10.5 t/hr, peaking at 18 t/hr in December — [Carbon Mapper](https://carbonmapper.org/articles/persistent-methane-super-emitters)

**Landfill and waste methane**
- Tanager-1 (Carbon Mapper/Planet/NASA JPL) first light, Sept 2024: a **4 km methane plume from a Karachi landfill**, about 1,200 kg CH4/hr — [NASA JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/first-greenhouse-gas-plumes-detected-with-nasa-designed-instrument/)
- Mumbai: **9 plumes from 7 landfills and dumpsites in one Tanager-1 scene** (Feb 9, 2025), totalling about 4,100 kg CH4/hr. Most sites were still emitting in an April 2026 re-observation, nearly a year later (published Jul 2026) — [Carbon Mapper Mumbai dispatch](https://carbonmapper.org/articles/mumbai-waste-sites)

**Fragile satellite capacity**
- **MethaneSAT** (EDF, launched Mar 4, 2024) **lost contact on June 20, 2025**. It was declared likely unrecoverable on July 2 after about 400 recovery attempts over 21 days, so a planned 5-year mission lasted about 15 months. The failure was traced to avionics or the power system. It had collected data over 41 oil and gas basins in 25 countries — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MethaneSAT); [NZ MBIE update](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/about/news/methanesat-update-july-2025); [EDF](https://www.edf.org/launch)
- The FY2026 US President's Budget Request proposed **ending NASA OCO-2 and OCO-3** (CO2 satellites; about $15M/yr to operate). NASA was told to plan "Phase F" closeout. The final outcome was not verified — [Eos](https://eos.org/research-and-developments/nasa-planning-for-unauthorized-shutdown-of-carbon-monitoring-satellites); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/New-White-House-proposal-could-terminate-NASA-satellites-tracking-CO2-and-crop-health.1080185.0.html)

**Biennial Transparency Reports (BTR1, due Dec 31, 2024)**
- By January 2025, **90 Parties** had submitted BTR1, including 58 developing countries (13 of them LDCs or SIDS) — [UNFCCC](https://unfccc.int/news/90-parties-submit-first-biennial-transparency-reports)
- By Feb 27, 2025, **105 Parties** had submitted (78 counting the EU as one), and **90 Non-Annex I submissions were still pending** — [Pingault & Martius via AGRIS/FAO](https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/69034553b901ffe5ca6519a3)
- A UNFCCC synthesis report covers BTRs and NIRs received by Apr 15, 2025 — [UNFCCC synthesis](https://unfccc.int/documents/651012)

### Inferences
- The gap is now less about detection and more about **closing the loop**: whether an alert was received, acknowledged, verified on the ground, repaired and re-checked. A public, timestamped record of "alert → response → fix → satellite re-check" could add accountability where response rates are 0–20%.
- Persistent emitters (Mumbai landfills, Turkmenistan flares) show that repeated observations over time matter more than one-off detections.
- Independent satellite capacity is concentrated in a few philanthropic or US-government missions, so it is a single point of failure (MethaneSAT loss, OCO cuts).

### Gaps
- Exact BTR1 submission count as of Oct 2026 was not found (latest found: Feb–Apr 2025). No systematic quality assessment of BTRs was found beyond the Pingault & Martius paper title.
- The UNEP press release and MARS snapshot could not be fetched (403). The 12% vs 13% response-rate figures cover different time windows.
- Final FY2026 status of OCO-2/OCO-3 was not verified.

## 2. Climate data for adaptation and the Global South: weather stations, early warnings, SOFF, loss & damage, parametric insurance

### Takeaway
Africa and small island states lack basic observations: station density is about **8 times below the WMO minimum**, and Chad has 1 of 33 required stations. Early-warning coverage has reached 60% of countries but only 43% of SIDS, with the 2027 deadline approaching. Index and parametric insurance depends on trusted local weather data, and **basis risk** (payouts that do not match real losses) remains its central weakness.

### Cited Findings
- WMO Africa regional director Joseph Mukabana: Africa has about **1 station per 26,000 km², eight times below the WMO minimum**. Germany has more reporting surface stations than all of Africa. About 54% of Africa's surface stations and 71% of its upper-air stations cannot capture accurate data [figure date not confirmed, likely OLDER] — [Down to Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/climate-change/54-of-africa-s-surface-weather-stations-71-of-its-upper-air-weather-stations-unable-to-capture-accurate-data-60685); [SciDev](https://www.scidev.net/global/?p=30954)
- GBON designation in Africa rose from 589 to 1,045 surface stations and from 38 to 50 upper-air stations (2023). Designation does not mean the stations are functioning or reporting — [WMO](https://wmo.int/media/magazine-article/africa-increases-designation-of-gbon-stations)
- SOFF gap analyses: **Chad has only 1 of 33 surface stations meeting GBON and no upper-air capacity** (May 2024). Ethiopia needs 29 surface and 5 upper-air stations and has no active upper-air reporting. Tanzania is building 9 new stations, upgrading 18 and adding 4 upper-air stations (2024–2027). Madagascar has about US$4.9M for 2025–2028 — [SOFF Chad prodoc](https://mptf.undp.org/sites/default/files/documents/2025-03/soff_chad_prodoc_wfp_redacted.pdf); [UNDP Ethiopia SOFF](https://www.adaptation-undp.org/projects/ethiopia-soff-investment-phase); [UNDP Tanzania](https://www.undp.org/tanzania/news/building-climate-resilience-tanzania-launches-soff-project); [UNDP Madagascar](https://adaptation-undp.org/projects/madagascar-soff-investment-phase)
- The SOFF 2025 annual report says **23 countries are in the Investment Phase**. In 2024 WMO said an extra US$120M was needed by June 2025 to meet demand — [SOFF Fund Report 2025](https://mptf.undp.org/sites/default/files/documents/2026-05/soff_fund_report_2025.pdf); [Meteorological Technology International](https://www.meteorologicaltechnologyinternational.com/?p=11434)
- Global Status of MHEWS 2025 (UNDRR/WMO, COP30, Nov 12, 2025): **119 countries (60%)** report a multi-hazard early warning system, up 113% in a decade. Coverage is 72% in Asia-Pacific, 51% in the Americas and Caribbean, and **43% in SIDS**. Fewer than one-third of countries have risk-knowledge capability. Disaster mortality is about 6 times lower in countries with comprehensive systems. The target is universal coverage by 2027 — [WMO news](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/early-warning-systems-reach-new-heights-critical-gaps-jeopardize-global-progress); [WMO publication](https://wmo.int/publication-series/global-status-of-multi-hazard-early-warning-systems-2025)
- Pacific: SPREP says the region is **losing one long-term climate dataset every two years**, which it calls a crisis caused by maintenance and sustainability problems despite more climate finance (Mar 2026) — [NIWA Pasifika Pānui](https://niwa.co.nz/pacific/pasifika-panui/issue-3-march-2026/sustainable-observation-networks)
- [OLDER, 2023] *Climate Change in the Pacific 2022* (SPC): data quality has declined since the 1990s. More than half of the 15 countries have only one or no station with a near-complete temperature record from 1980 to 2020, and some national networks shrank by more than 80% in 30 years — [Asia Pacific Report](https://asiapacificreport.nz/2023/02/09/climate-data-from-pacific-has-deteriorated-says-new-report/)
- Loss & Damage Fund (FRLD): the Barbados Implementation Modalities (BIM) start-up phase has **US$250M**, with grants of $5–20M per request. Requests opened Dec 15, 2025 for six months, with at least 50% for SIDS and LDCs. No disbursement data was found — [The New Humanitarian](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/analysis/2025/11/18/big-questions-loss-and-damage-funds-250m-trial-run); [Barbados MFA](https://www.foreign.gov.bb/barbados-implementation-modalities-bim-are-mobilised); [Caribbean Today](https://www.caribbeantoday.com/sections/regional/caribbean-countries-to-benefit-from-new-loss-and-damage-initiative)

**Parametric and index insurance**
- [OLDER, 2019–20] CPI Climate Finance Lab, "Blockchain Climate Risk Crop Insurance" (Sprout Insure + ACRE Africa + Etherisc): smart-contract policies indexed to local weather, with automatic payouts and premiums as low as $0.50. It targeted 1.2M Kenyan farmers from April 2020. **Only 20% of smallholders in developing countries, and 3% in sub-Saharan Africa, have agricultural insurance.** No published pilot results were found — [CPI](https://www.climatepolicyinitiative.org/publication/blockchain-climate-risk-crop-insurance/); [Lab overview PDF](https://www.climatefinancelab.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Blockchain_instrument-overview.pdf); [Etherisc + Chainlink Kenya](https://blog.etherisc.com/etherisc-teams-up-with-chainlink-to-deliver-crop-insurance-in-kenya-137e433c29dc)
- Basis risk evidence: Index-Based Livestock Insurance (IBLI) covers only covariate shocks. Marsabit pastoralists face large idiosyncratic risk that the index cannot cover, and with weakly correlated indices the insurance "represents a gamble" — [Jensen, Barrett & Mude, Cornell](https://barrett.dyson.cornell.edu/files/papers/150824_JensenBarrettMude_FavorableImpactsKenya.pdf). For Kenyan maize, Stigler & Lobell find basis risk "high in practice" and split it into zonal and design risk — [arXiv 2111.08601](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.08601). Columbia SIPA and the WFP R4 programme (Ethiopia) found "downside basis risk", where farmers lose heavily but get little or nothing, and propose complementary risk funds — [Columbia SIPA](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sipa-education/capstone-workshops/when-does-index-insurance-provide-good-coverage)
- Arbol uses **dClimate** (a decentralized climate-data network it co-founded) to price parametric cover. Wind-triggered contracts use dClimate data plus Chainlink oracles. Arbol reported $250M gross written premium in 2023, raised a $60M Series B in April 2024, and launched parametric wildfire cover (Lloyd's Somers Syndicate, up to $10M) in 2025. These are company claims — [Arbol/dClimate intro](https://www.arbol.io/post/an-introduction-to-dclimate-a-decentralized-network-for-climate-data-2); [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/arbols-climate-data-network-dclimate-gets-mark-cuban-investment/); [Arbol pricing](https://arbol.io/post/how-parametric-insurance-pricing-works)

### Inferences
- Parametric payouts are only as trustworthy as the nearest data point. Where stations are 100–200 km apart, putting the payout logic on a blockchain does not fix basis risk. **Denser, verifiable local observations** combined with satellite data and farmer-reported loss data are the missing layer.
- Loss & Damage Fund requests, early warnings and SOFF all need **verifiable baseline and loss data**. Small states cannot produce it themselves, and new hardware decays without maintenance (the Pacific example).

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 continent-wide count of non-functioning African stations was found. The "8x" ratio and the 54%/71% figures appear older and could not be dated precisely.
- No published outcome data was found for the ACRE/Etherisc blockchain pilot (farmers insured, payout accuracy).
- No FRLD disbursement figures as of Oct 2026 were found.

## 3. Air quality, citizen science, water quality and biodiversity monitoring

### Takeaway
About a third of countries (home to nearly 1 billion people) have **no government air-quality monitoring**, and only about a quarter share data openly. Low-cost sensors fill gaps but need calibration, humidity being the largest error source. Trust in citizen data is the main barrier to official use. Biodiversity MRV is fragmented: more than 30 credit standards, no agreed metric, and a market under $200M.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAQ *Open Air Quality Data: The Global Landscape 2024*: **36% of countries, with nearly 1 billion people, do not monitor air quality**, and most are low or lower-middle income. In the most populous of these countries, air pollution is a top-7 risk factor for death and disability. **Only just over one-quarter of countries provide full, easy public access** to useful air-quality data. There has been only a small improvement since 2022 — [CCAC/OpenAQ](https://www.ccacoalition.org/resources/open-air-quality-data-global-landscape); [OpenAQ publications](https://openaq.org/about/initiatives/publications)
- [OLDER, 2020] In just over half of 212 countries, the government produced no air-quality data — [India Environment Portal](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/reports-and-documents/open-air-quality-data-the-global-state-of-play)
- State of Global Air 2024: air pollution caused **8.1 million deaths in 2021** — [State of Global Air](https://stateofglobalair.org/resources/report/state-global-air-report-2024)
- Low-cost sensors: **relative humidity is the main source of PurpleAir PM2.5 error**. EPA's national (Barkjohn) correction lets corrected PurpleAir data appear on AirNow. Regional models for the humid US Southeast cut error by 16–23%, with nonlinear effects above about 50% RH. Dhaka (2025) needed its own local calibration — [PMC: PurpleAir high-RH calibration](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11900072/); [AMT 2024 / DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/c8b8d51a526845cfac06a00227df7245)
- [OLDER, 2021] Sensor.Community: more than 14,000 sensors in over 70 countries and more than 13 billion data points, fully open — [SotM Africa 2021](https://pretalx.com/sotm-africa-2021/speaker/89XJJW/)
- Water and citizen data trust: Atlantic DataStream (Canada) fingerprints each dataset at upload so users can verify it has not been altered. It holds more than 3M observations from 120+ organizations at 7,000+ sites. The LoRaTRUST paper (UPC) says decision-makers get little value from IoT sensor data because "it lacks sufficient guarantees of trustworthiness" — [IISD](https://iisd.org/blog/citizen-science-freshwater); [WWF-Canada](https://techhub.wwf.ca/?p=541); [UPC LoRaTRUST](https://dsg.ac.upc.edu/bibcite/reference/648)
- No peer-reviewed study comparing tamper rates or data quality with and without blockchain was found. A ledger proves that a record was not changed after upload, but not that the sensor read correctly (the same search found no evaluation of sensor-level attacks) — [UZH thesis](https://files.ifi.uzh.ch/CSG/staff/Rafati/Sanjiv-Jha-MA.pdf)
- Biodiversity MRV:
  - Bell & Malerba 2025 (*Biodiversity and Conservation*): passive acoustic monitoring (PAM) recorded **about 70 times more detections** and had the lowest cost per species over 5+ campaigns, but covers only vocalising taxa. eDNA was the fastest method and the only one to capture invertebrates, but its costs rose quickly with repeated sampling — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10531-025-03083-0)
  - PAM rarely delivers consistent data at scale because of equipment and maintenance costs, data-management complexity and slow uptake of standard protocols. TABMON is a 108-sensor network across 4 European countries — [TABMON](https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/802006/Cretois-2025-TABMON-A.pdf); [arXiv 2509.02201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.02201)
  - Biodiversity credits: under **$200M in annual transactions, more than 30 competing standards, and no agreed metric**. MRV companies have faced an "MRV winter" of funding — [Sustainable Atlas](https://sustainableatlas.org/post/myths-vs-realities-biodiversity-credits-nature-markets-what-the-evidence-actuall-3324); [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/490394/). [OLDER, 2023] WEF put the voluntary market at about $8M — [WEF](https://cn.weforum.org/publications/biodiversity-credits-demand-drivers-and-guidance-on-early-use/)

### Inferences
- The most practical "trust layer" for citizen sensors is **calibration provenance**: which reference monitor a sensor was co-located with, which correction model and version was applied, and a device-signed hash. A tamper-evident ledger alone is not enough.
- Low-income countries with no monitoring are where low-cost sensors combined with satellite AOD and AI correction add the most value, and where local co-location reference data is scarcest.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–26) Sensor.Community or PurpleAir network size was found.
- No rigorous evaluation of blockchain-anchored environmental sensor data in official regulatory use was found.

## 4. Climate data infrastructure, openness, and the 2025 US federal data removals and rescue

### Takeaway
In 2025 the US dismantled or downgraded core climate data products: climate.gov, the billion-dollar disasters database, NOAA snow and ice products, a proposed end to EPA's GHGRP, and proposed ends to the OCO satellites. Volunteer, academic and NGO rescue efforts (Harvard LIL, PEDP, Data Rescue Project, Climate Central, Climate.us) responded quickly. **This is a concrete, current case for tamper-evident, multi-custodian, verifiable climate data archives.**

### Cited Findings
- **climate.gov**: content staff were cut in Feb 2025 (probationary purge) and May 31, 2025 (the 10-person contract team was defunded). From about June 24, 2025 the site redirects to noaa.gov. Former staff relaunched it as **Climate.us** (Nieman Lab, Sept 2026) — [SAN](https://san.com/cc/noaa-redirects-climate-gov-to-its-homepage/); [Eos](https://eos.org/?p=237300); [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/09/the-trump-administration-killed-climate-gov-a-new-website-is-resurrecting-it/)
- **Billion-Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters** database: NOAA retired it in May 2025, with no updates after 2024 and the 1980–2024 archive kept. Climate Central hired its former lead, Adam Smith, to revive it — [ClickOnDetroit/AP](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/national/2025/05/08/us-retires-database-tracking-billions-of-dollars-of-climate-change-fueled-weather-damage/); [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2025/08/06/834819.htm)
- **NOAA/NCEI decommissionings (2025)**: snow and ice products cut to "Basic" support from May 5, 2025 (Sea Ice Index, SNODAS, Glacier Photograph Collection, World Glacier Inventory, and others). 14 datasets on earthquakes, marine and coastal science were to be decommissioned by May 2025, and more followed. This compares with only 7 decommissionings in 2024 and 6 in 2023 — [NSIDC notice](https://nsidc.org/data/user-resources/data-announcements/user-notice-level-service-update-data-products); [Eos](https://eos.org/research-and-developments/noaa-datasets-will-soon-disappear); [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/threats-to-noaa-funded-snow-and-ice-data-products-alarm-polar-scientists); [Columbia Sabin Center](https://climate.law.columbia.edu/content/noaa-decommissions-sea-ice-datasets)
- **EPA GHGRP**: on Sept 12, 2025 EPA proposed ending reporting for all source categories except petroleum and natural gas, with most of that deferred to 2034. A final rule (Feb 27, 2026) moved the 2025 reporting deadline to Oct 30, 2026. **The repeal was not final as of the latest reporting**, so the GHGRP stays in effect. Some states (e.g., New York) are continuing their own reporting — [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/epa-will-halt-greenhouse-gas-reporting-for-big-polluters/); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/epa-reconsiders-greenhouse-gas-reporting-program); [Lion](https://www.lion.com/lion-news/march-2026/deadline-extended-greenhouse-gas-reporting-program); [Environment+Energy Leader](https://environmentenergyleader.com/stories/new-york-emissions-reporting-continues-despite-epa-rollback,142881)

**Data rescue**
- **Harvard Law School Library Innovation Lab data.gov archive** (Feb 2025, on Source Cooperative): **311,000+ datasets, about 17.9 TB**, updated daily, with **digital signatures and metadata so records can be authenticated**. A search interface was added in Oct 2025 — [Harvard LIL](https://lil.law.harvard.edu/blog/2025/02/06/announcing-data-gov-archive); [InfoDocket](https://www.infodocket.com/2025/10/10/research-tools-the-library-innovation-lab-at-harvard-law-school-announces-launch-of-data-gov-archive-search-access-to-311000-datasets-17-9-terabytes-of-data/)
- **Public Environmental Data Partners (PEDP)** (with EDGI) rescued CEJST and EJScreen-type tools (Jan 2025) and rebuilt the deleted HIFLD infrastructure map as "HIFLD Next". **PANGAEA** archived NOAA datasets slated for decommissioning. DataLumos (University of Michigan) hosts the Climate Mirror. About 2,000 records reportedly went missing from government datasets (single-interview estimate) — [Edinburgh data blog](https://libraryblogs.is.ed.ac.uk/datablog/?p=4247); [PANGAEA wiki](https://wiki.pangaea.de/wiki/Data_Rescue); [Yale e360, Eric Nost interview](https://e360.yale.edu/features/eric-nost-interview); [Data Rescue Project](https://datarescueproject.org/current-efforts)

**Open infrastructure**
- **OS-Climate** joined the Linux Foundation's **FINOS** in June 2024 as a directed fund, bringing Data Commons, Portfolio Alignment, Physical Risk and Transition Scenario tools — [Linux Foundation](https://linuxfoundation.org/press/os-climate-joins-forces-with-finos-to-enable-industry-wide-open-collaboration-for-climate-and-sustainability-aligned-finance)
- Copernicus: ESA spent about €2.3B on Earth observation in 2024, more than NASA, and Copernicus delivers **more than 25 TB of open data a day**. ESA's EO director warned that a nearly 50% cut to US science would reduce cooperation on calibration, validation and climate-data exchange — [EMS Now](https://www.emsnow.com/?p=50638); [Futura Sciences](https://www.futura-sciences.com/sciences/actualites/astronautique-budget-nasa-coupe-missions-annulees-agence-spatiale-europeenne-nous-repond-avenir-science-climat-125333/)

### Inferences
- The 2025 US experience shows that "authoritative" data can disappear or freeze for political reasons. Users such as insurers, researchers, state governments and courts need to **prove that a copy is identical to the original**. Content hashing, signatures and multi-site mirrors (as Harvard LIL does) are in demand, and a blockchain timestamp or notary layer over rescued datasets is a credible, non-hype use case.
- With the GHGRP repeal pending, US facility-level emissions disclosure may shift to states and independent satellite or AI inventories such as Climate TRACE and Carbon Mapper.

### Gaps
- Final status of the GHGRP repeal as of Oct 2026 was not confirmed.
- No consolidated count of removed US climate datasets was found (EDGI tracking reports were not retrieved).
- Google Earth Engine, NASA Earthdata and Data Commons access or pricing changes in 2025–26 were not researched.

## 5. City and subnational climate data

### Takeaway
Most cities cannot produce a GHG inventory. Open Earth Foundation cites **only about 5% of cities** having the staff and expertise to quantify their emissions. African city inventories are sparse and not comparable.

### Cited Findings
- About **5% of cities worldwide** have the people and expertise to quantify their GHG emissions. **CityCatalyst** (Open Earth Foundation) uses machine learning on satellite imagery to fill this gap. The source is a secondary profile — [McGovern / OpenEarth profile](https://learn.mcgovern.org/OpenEarth-Foundation-18e70021ed8c8053a08dd78c82e43f4f)
- 2024 Africa study: **270 inventories from 137 cities in 54 countries**. Inventories lack standard protocols, are often not comparable, and suffer from data scarcity and weak local-government engagement — [University of Groningen IREES](https://www.rug.nl/research/irees/news/2024/2810-the-landscape-of-city-level-ghg-emission-accounts-in-africa)
- Local governments are "relatively new to MRV" (Thai pilot) — [APN](https://www.apn-gcr.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/10.30852_sb.2015.20.pdf)
- [OLDER, 2014–15] Among cities reporting to CDP in 2014, 77% had city-wide inventories (a self-selected sample). ICLEI's carbonn registry held 608 entities in 62 countries by 2015 — [ICLEI cCR 2015](https://e-lib.iclei.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/cCR2015_5Year_Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Small and medium cities, including Taiwan's townships and Türkiye's municipalities, need **low-effort, auditable inventory tooling** that pre-fills from satellite, Climate TRACE and utility data and lets officials attest to or correct entries with an audit trail.

### Gaps
- No current global share of cities without an inventory was found from CDP-ICLEI or GCoM 2025 data. The CityCatalyst user list was not found.

## 6. AI for climate monitoring: weather models, wildfire, floods

### Takeaway
AI weather models (GenCast, AIFS, Aurora) now match or beat physics models on most benchmarks at a fraction of the cost, and ECMWF runs AIFS operationally. But they **underestimate record-breaking extremes**, are trained on reanalysis that is thin where observations are sparse, and still depend on observations to start each forecast. AI does not remove the need for ground stations.

### Cited Findings
- **GenCast** (DeepMind, Nature, Dec 2024): beat ECMWF's ENS on **97.2% of 1,320 targets** (99.8% beyond 36 hours) and produces a 15-day ensemble in about 8 minutes on one TPU v5. It was trained to 2018 and tested on 2019 — [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/gencast-predicts-weather-and-the-risks-of-extreme-conditions-with-sota-accuracy/)
- **Aurora** (Microsoft, Nature, May 2025): a foundation model trained on more than 1M hours of data. It outperforms operational forecasts for air quality, ocean waves, tropical cyclone tracks and high-resolution weather — [Nature via RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v641y2025i8065d10.1038_s41586-025-09005-y.html); [Azure Labs](https://labs.ai.azure.com/innovations/aurora/)
- **ECMWF AIFS** went operational on **Feb 25, 2025** (Single). The AIFS ensemble followed on July 1, 2025, and v2 models on May 12, 2026. ECMWF reports up to 20% better tropical cyclone tracks and about 1,000 times less energy per forecast — [ECMWF](https://www.ecmwf.int/node/29308)
- Limits: a University of Geneva study found GraphCast, Pangu and FuXi "systematically wrong" on record-breaking events, underestimating cold spells, heat waves and winds, and called for hybrid systems — [Anadolu Agency](https://aa.com.tr/en/europe/ai-weather-models-struggle-to-predict-extreme-events-swiss-study/3923211). AIFS shows weaknesses in precipitation, extremes and local wind — [KNMI](https://www.knmi.nl/research/weather-climate-models/projects/developing-a-new-high-resolution-weather-prediction-model-using-ai). An Africa early-warning preprint (2026) notes AI models depend on the quality of upstream GFS initial data — [arXiv 2602.17726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17726). Oxford comment (Aug 2026): AI forecasts "currently have significant limitations" — [Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-08-19-expert-comment-more-extreme-weather-is-coming-can-ai-help-us-to-better-prepare-for)
- **Google Flood Hub**: riverine forecasts covering **about 700M people in 100 countries** with verified data, and up to 150 countries via "virtual gauges" where gauge data is scarce. It gives up to 7 days' lead time, **covers riverine floods only (no flash floods)**, and offers offline alerts via the Red Cross — [Google blog, Nov 2024](https://blog.google/technology/ai/expanding-flood-forecasting-coverage-helping-partners/); [Google Flood Forecasting](https://sites.research.google/floodforecasting)
- **FireSat** (Earth Fire Alliance, Google Research, Muon Space): a prototype launched in March 2025, with 50+ satellites planned to detect **5×5 m fires within 20 minutes**. Google.org gave $13M. The first 3 operational satellites reportedly launched on Transporter-17 (Jul 2026, secondary source) — [Google blog](https://blog.google/feed/firesat-first-satellite-launch/); [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/earth-fire-alliance-launches-operational-firesat-satellites-244abd23)

### Inferences
- AI makes forecasts cheap. The scarce inputs are now **ground-truth observations** (for initialization, verification and local downscaling) and **trust and provenance** (which model version, which inputs). Flood Hub's "virtual gauges" show how data-starved regions are handled.
- There is room for tools that **verify AI forecasts locally** (compare against community stations, publish skill scores) and that bring flash floods and landslide-dam events into scope, since Flood Hub covers only rivers.

### Gaps
- No direct verification of GraphCast or AIFS skill over Africa or the Pacific was found. FireSat operational status was only confirmed through a secondary source.

## 7. Blockchain and DePIN for environmental data integrity: adoption vs. hype

### Takeaway
Weather and air DePINs are real but small. WeatherXM has about 4,000–7,000 stations, and commercial buyers such as meteoblue filter its data by quality score. PlanetWatch's air-quality network was absorbed into Ambient after token problems. Across DePIN, 2025 on-chain revenue was about **$72M against roughly $10B market cap**, and sensor networks are a minor share. The durable value is **device-signed provenance and quality scoring**, not token rewards.

### Cited Findings
- **WeatherXM**:
  - Stations carry a secure element that cryptographically signs data. A daily Quality-of-Data (QoD) score sets token rewards. Indoor Station Detection (Sept 2025) flags stations installed indoors — [Synoptic](https://synopticdata.com/blog/decentralized-weather-data-synoptic-adds-weatherxms-global-data-to-its-platform/); [DePIN Hub](https://9o1hbtdj3x.depinhub.io/news/weather-xm-enhances-qo-d-algorithm-for-community-powered-weather-network-24102)
  - Scale: 4,000+ stations in 80 countries (2024), and 7,000+ stations per Synoptic. The Block reported 7,000 sold and 5,000 deployed. **Figures conflict, and no independent accuracy audit was found** — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/277530/weatherxm-surpasses-1-5m-station-days-as-they-prepare-for-mainnet-rewards); [Arbitrum blog](https://blog.arbitrum.io/how-weatherxm-is-paving-the-way-for-depin-on-arbitrum/)
  - meteoblue integrated WeatherXM in Aug 2025 but **filters by QoD**, which reduces the number of observations — [meteoblue changelog](https://business.meteoblue.com/changelog/weatherxm-station-quality-filtering)
  - "Targeted Rollouts" (Nov 2025): stations are deployed where demand is mapped, funded by supporter NFTs (75% of rewards to funders, 25% to deployers). 320 stations went up in South Africa in 90 days for utilities, insurers and municipalities (company-sourced) — [DePIN Hub](https://depinhub.io/blog/weatherxms-beaver-nfts-test-a-new-model-for-depin-funding-through-targeted-rollouts/)
- **PlanetWatch** (Algorand air-quality DePIN) was acquired by Solana-based Ambient in May 2024. The PLANETS token was replaced and the directory lists the project as "no longer active" — [The Block](https://theblock.co/post/292971/solana-depin-ambient-funding-token-acquires-planetwatch); [DePIN Hub](https://9o1hbtdj3x.depinhub.io/projects/planetwatch)
- **Silencio** (smartphone noise DePIN, on peaq): user counts range from 40k to 1.1M depending on source and date, and no paying customers or revenue were found — [DePIN Hub](https://depinhub.io/projects/silencio/)
- Sector: according to Messari, DePIN tokens launched 2018–2022 are **94–99% below their highs**, while the sector (~$10B market cap) earned about **$72M in on-chain revenue in 2025**. Leaders trade at 10–25 times revenue, compared with more than 1,000 times in 2021. Paying-demand examples are mostly bandwidth, compute, energy and GEODNET RTK positioning (about $3M annualized fee revenue in Jan 2025), not environmental sensing — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/356349/depin-tokens-lag-revenues-rise-fundamentals); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/depin-revenues-grow-despite-token-losses)
- Blockchain-anchored data in use: Atlantic DataStream fingerprinting (see Q3); Arbol/dClimate with Chainlink oracles (see Q2); the Harvard LIL signed archive is not blockchain but has the same integrity goal (see Q4).

### Inferences
- What judges are likely to find credible: (1) a **hardware or device signature plus a calibration and QA record anchored on-chain**; (2) **notarizing public datasets** against deletion or alteration; (3) **automatic, auditable triggers** for parametric payouts or alerts. Token-incentivized deployment alone has a poor track record (PlanetWatch, low sensor revenue).
- Quality scoring must be explicit and checkable by data buyers, as meteoblue's QoD filtering shows.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed accuracy comparison of WeatherXM against WMO-grade stations was found. Ambient network status in 2026 is unknown.

## 9. Taiwan and Asia angle

### Takeaway
Taiwan is a strong civic-tech case: LASS/AirBox, Civil IoT Taiwan with more than 10,000 government micro-sensors, g0v Disfactory, and TCCIP downscaled climate data. Its 2025 pain point was **disaster information during cascading hazards**: the Mataian River (馬太鞍溪) landslide-dam overflow killed about 19 people in Guangfu (光復鄉) even though the dam had been watched for two months. Pacific islands are losing long-term climate records.

### Cited Findings
- **Civil IoT Taiwan (民生公共物聯網)**: run jointly by the National Science and Technology Council, Ministry of Environment, Ministry of the Interior, Ministry of Transportation, Ministry of Digital Affairs, Ministry of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Agriculture and Academia Sinica, as part of the Forward-looking Infrastructure Development Program (前瞻基礎建設). It covers four domains: air, water, earthquakes and disaster prevention. The government has deployed **more than 10,000 air-pollution micro-sensors** (document undated). The Ministry of Environment cautions that simple sensors' PM2.5 readings can differ from standard monitoring stations — [Executive Yuan](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/9277F759E41CCD91/cc905518-12e7-476d-b83b-fd2ddc1712d5); [smartcity.org.tw](https://smartcity.org.tw/b2b/upload/ecatalog/supplier_pdf_en_1212_450.pdf); [data.gov.tw Tainan micro-sensor dataset](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/128015)
- **LASS / AirBox (空氣盒子)**: a maker community working with Academia Sinica. [OLDER] It had about 3,000–4,000+ boxes around 2018–19, and Taiwan has been described as having the world's highest density of micro-sensors. In 2019 Audrey Tang cited more than 2,000 civil-society air detectors — [CivicTech Guide](https://civictech.guide/listing/location-aware-sensing-system-lass-environmental-sensor-network); [The News Lens](https://www.thenewslens.com/article/108267); [archive.tw](https://archive.tw/speech/219045)
- **g0v Disfactory (農地違章工廠回報)** (with Citizen of the Earth, Taiwan): by 2024, **3,215 citizen reports led to 859 formal complaints** to local governments. Taiwan loses about 1,500 ha of farmland a year to illegal use. It helped bring the 2019 amendment to the Factory Management Act ("report and demolish" for new illegal factories built after May 20, 2016) and regular publication of enforcement progress. More than 70 contributors have taken part — [CET report 2024](https://cet-taiwan.org/sites/cet-taiwan.org/files/20240828%E3%80%90%E8%AA%BF%E6%9F%A5%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A%E3%80%91%E5%85%AD%E7%B8%A3%E5%B8%82%E8%BE%B2%E5%9C%B0%E9%81%95%E7%AB%A0%E5%B7%A5%E5%BB%A0%E6%9F%A5%E8%99%95%E6%88%90%E6%95%88%E8%88%87%E9%AB%94%E8%B3%AA%E8%A8%BA%E6%96%B7%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A.pdf); [CET](https://www.cet-taiwan.org/node/4071); [COSCUP 2022](https://coscup.org/2022//en/session/WFSF9Z)
- **TCCIP and the National Climate Change Science Report**: the *National Climate Change Science Report 2024* (國家氣候變遷科學報告2024) was published jointly by NSTC and the Ministry of Environment under the 2023 Climate Change Response Act. TCCIP released AR6 1 km statistical downscaling in 2025, with dynamical downscaling and ocean data planned for 2026. The third National Climate Change Adaptation Action Plan runs 2023–2026 — [TCCIP 2025 slides](https://tccip.ncdr.nat.gov.tw/upload/activity_agenda/20250804113244.pdf); [TCCIP Science Report](https://tccip.ncdr.nat.gov.tw/ScientificReport2024/dl_intro.aspx); [Want Rich / China Times](https://wantrich.chinatimes.com/news/20260512900701-420101)
- **Mataian River landslide-dam disaster (馬太鞍溪堰塞湖)**: the dam formed after Typhoon Wipha (薇帕) in July 2025, about 250 m high with up to 100 million m³ of storage. Typhoon Podul (楊柳) forced an evacuation of 697 people in August. Ahead of Typhoon Ragasa (樺加沙), the Ministry of the Interior ordered 1,800 households evacuated on the night of Sept 21, with a red alert on Sept 22 covering 1,837 households (8,524 actual residents). **The dam overflowed at about 14:50 on Sept 23, earlier than projected because of heavy rain**, destroying the Mataian bridge and flooding most of Guangfu town. The government later reported **19 dead and 5 missing**, more than 2,000 households and about 600 ha of farmland buried. A yellow alert recurred on Nov 9, 2025 — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.com.tw/article/category/183027/post/202509230052/); [Mirror Media](https://www.mirrormedia.mg/story/20250924edi040); [kiang HackMD timeline](https://hackmd.io/@kiang/Sy4gDsMneg); [President's Office doc](https://www.president.gov.tw/File/Doc/3e4af3fb-e64d-42e0-bb9d-951ca80e25b4); [TVBS](https://news.tvbs.com.tw/politics/2999005)
- Asia and the Pacific: early-warning coverage is 72% in Asia-Pacific, the highest region, but only 43% in SIDS (Q2). The Pacific is losing one long-term climate dataset every two years (Q2). Mumbai and Karachi landfills are major methane point sources (Q1).

### Inferences
- The Mataian case shows that alerts existed, but **the timing and uncertainty of overflow forecasts, and how they reached residents**, failed under rapidly changing rainfall. Citizen civic-tech (the HackMD timeline by kiang, a g0v contributor) filled information gaps. Useful directions: verifiable, real-time hazard sensor feeds (water level, rainfall, dam monitoring) with public audit logs of when each warning was issued and to whom.
- Taiwan's mixed government and citizen sensor network is a ready testbed for **calibration-provenance and data-integrity layers** that could then be exported to Pacific or Southeast Asian partners.

- Official 2025–26 Civil IoT sensor counts and current LASS station numbers were not found.
- Ministry of Environment open-data platform metrics and the typhoon and flood data architecture were not detailed.
- No formal post-disaster review of the Mataian warning chain was retrieved.

## 10. Concrete unmet needs: who needs trustworthy climate data but cannot get it, and what they would do with it

### Takeaway
The clearest unmet needs combine **missing data** (Global South stations, city inventories, air quality in a third of countries) with **missing trust and accountability** (unanswered methane alerts, deletable government datasets, unverifiable citizen sensors, basis-risk-prone insurance triggers).

### Cited Findings (synthesis of evidence above)
- **Methane accountability gap**: about 88–90% of alerts are unanswered and the US answered none of 138. Regulators, investors and the public need a verifiable record of which alerts were sent, which were fixed, and satellite re-confirmation — [IISD](https://sdg.iisd.org/news/un-report-calls-for-scaling-action-on-methane-through-data-driven-solutions/); [Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/world/arid-41904757.html)
- **Smallholder farmers and herders**: only 3% of sub-Saharan smallholders are insured, and basis risk is high. They need dense, trusted local weather data and a transparent payout trigger — [CPI Lab](https://www.climatefinancelab.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Blockchain_instrument-overview.pdf); [arXiv Stigler & Lobell](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.08601)
- **National meteorological services in LDCs and SIDS**: Chad has 1 of 33 required stations, the Pacific is losing a dataset every two years, and SIDS early-warning coverage is 43%. They need low-cost, maintainable observation networks whose data meets WMO/GBON standards and can be shared — [SOFF Chad](https://mptf.undp.org/sites/default/files/documents/2025-03/soff_chad_prodoc_wfp_redacted.pdf); [NIWA](https://niwa.co.nz/pacific/pasifika-panui/issue-3-march-2026/sustainable-observation-networks); [WMO](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/early-warning-systems-reach-new-heights-critical-gaps-jeopardize-global-progress)
- **People in the 36% of countries with no air-quality monitoring** (about 1 billion people) need calibrated low-cost sensor data that officials will accept — [OpenAQ/CCAC](https://www.ccacoalition.org/resources/open-air-quality-data-global-landscape)
- **Researchers, insurers, US states and courts** need provable copies of federal climate data after the 2025 removals — [Harvard LIL](https://lil.law.harvard.edu/blog/2025/02/06/announcing-data-gov-archive); [NSIDC](https://nsidc.org/data/user-resources/data-announcements/user-notice-level-service-update-data-products)
- **About 95% of cities** lack in-house GHG-inventory capacity — [McGovern/OEF](https://learn.mcgovern.org/OpenEarth-Foundation-18e70021ed8c8053a08dd78c82e43f4f)
- **Loss & Damage Fund applicants** (≥50% of the $250M is earmarked for SIDS and LDCs) need loss evidence and baselines — [The New Humanitarian](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/analysis/2025/11/18/big-questions-loss-and-damage-funds-250m-trial-run)
- **Residents downstream of cascading hazards** (Guangfu, Hualien) need transparent, timely hazard data and an auditable warning chain — [Mirror Media](https://www.mirrormedia.mg/story/20250924edi040)

### Inferences (candidate hackathon directions, grounded in the above)
1. **Methane Alert Accountability Ledger**: ingest public MARS, Carbon Mapper and Climate TRACE detections, timestamp each alert on-chain, track operator responses, and use AI to re-check satellite scenes for persistence. Builds on the data showing 0–20% response rates.
2. **Signed Climate Data Notary / Mirror**: hash and timestamp public climate datasets (NOAA, EPA, Taiwan Ministry of Environment, Copernicus) across many custodians, so anyone can verify a copy and detect silent edits. A direct response to the 2025 US removals.
3. **Calibrated Citizen Sensor Provenance**: device-signed readings plus on-chain records of co-location and correction model, with an AI QA score, so citizen data from LASS, Sensor.Community or WeatherXM-type networks is acceptable to agencies. Addresses humidity bias and the "lacks guarantees" barrier.
4. **Parametric Trigger with Basis-Risk Transparency**: combine station, satellite and crowd-reported loss data, publish trigger computations, and fund a complementary risk pool for downside basis risk.
5. **Cascading-hazard Warning Audit Trail** (Taiwan or Pacific pilot): log sensor readings, model forecasts and warnings issued, with times and recipients, for landslide dams and flash floods, which Google Flood Hub does not cover.
6. **City Inventory Copilot**: pre-fill a GPC inventory from open data and AI for small cities, with official attestation and a change log.

### Gaps
- No user-research data (surveys of farmers, met services or city officials) was found quantifying willingness to pay or adopt. Candidate directions are inferences, not validated demand.
