# Hackathon Topic Saturation: what other blockchain + AI climate teams build (for IEEE ClimateChain Global Hackathon 2026)

Method note (applies to all sections): The main dataset is a sample of about 199 Devpost project records (about 120 unique climate-related projects after removing duplicate and resubmitted entries). I pulled them on 2026-10-14 from Devpost's public software search JSON for 16 climate/blockchain queries: "carbon credit blockchain", "climate blockchain", "sustainability blockchain", "renewable energy blockchain", "carbon footprint blockchain", "supply chain sustainability blockchain", "recycling blockchain", "energy trading", "ESG blockchain", "emissions blockchain", "carbon offset", "climate AI web3", "solar token", "green token", "environmental monitoring blockchain" and "waste blockchain". The query pattern was `https://devpost.com/software/search?query=...`. Only the first page (24 results) of each query was captured, because Devpost then rate-limited the session (HTTP 429, then a Cloudflare challenge, which I did not try to bypass). The sample therefore leans toward the most relevant or recent items (mostly 2025–2026) and is not a census. A second dataset comes from ETHGlobal showcase title searches (`https://ethglobal.com/showcase?q=...`), which match project titles only. A third comes from official winner announcements (Hedera, Chainlink). Counts below are approximate counts within these samples, not population totals.

## Q1. What themes appear in comparable hackathon galleries (2023–2026), and how often? (frequency table)

### Takeaway
The galleries are dominated by four themes. Ranked by frequency: (1) carbon-credit verification, tokenization or marketplace, about 20% of climate projects; (2) personal or SME carbon-footprint trackers with token rewards, about 15%; (3) renewable or P2P solar energy trading and grid AI, about 15%; (4) generic supply-chain/agri traceability and recycling/waste reward tokens, about 10% each. Green-lending/ESG-escrow and climate-data/disaster tools fill most of the rest. Narrow, regulation-specific themes (CBAM, Article 6, F-gas, SAF book-and-claim, 24/7 matching, mass balance) are close to absent.

### Inferences
- The organizer's suggested-build lists overlap almost one-to-one with the most saturated gallery themes. Many of the 804 registrants (many probably students or first-time builders) are likely to copy these bullets. Expect the largest clusters at ClimateChain to be (a) carbon credit verification/marketplace with "double counting prevention" smart contracts, (b) P2P solar trading or tokenized clean-energy incentives, (c) DPP/product lifecycle or recycling-traceability reward tokens, and (d) IoT air-quality or satellite-deforestation monitoring dashboards.
- Rough projection, labeled as an inference: Devpost hackathons usually see a fraction of registrants submit, and teams are 2–5 people, so 804 registrants might yield on the order of 100–250 submissions. Applying the sample shares, plus extra weight for the organizer prompts, gives ballpark figures of 30–60 carbon-credit/registry/MRV projects, 20–40 P2P energy or REC projects, 15–35 traceability, DPP or recycling projects, and 15–30 footprint-tracker-with-tokens projects. These are estimates, not observed counts.
- "AI + satellite (Sentinel-2/Google Earth Engine) + on-chain carbon credit" is now its own saturated sub-pattern. CanopyProof, Carbon Credit Verifier, Carbon Credit Allocation and Verifier, Bluereach AI, Proof of Carbon and AgroGreenBits all appear in a single first page of results. "Satellite-verified credits" is no longer a differentiator.
- The duplicate-entry pattern (the same team submitting the same project to many hackathons) means judges with Devpost experience have probably seen generic carbon marketplaces many times.

### Gaps
- I could not page past the first 24 results per query, or run winner-only (`is:winner`) filters at scale, because Devpost rate-limited the session (HTTP 429, then a Cloudflare challenge). Shares are estimated from a non-random sample.
- DoraHacks BUIDL galleries, Gitcoin climate rounds, Colosseum/Solana, Celo, Algorand and Stellar galleries were not scanned directly. Web searches for them returned only generic ReFi explainers, not gallery data.
- ETHGlobal search matches titles only, so its counts undercount projects whose titles lack the keyword.

### Inferences
- The least-duplicated niches with zero hackathon precedent found are CBAM embedded-emissions passports, Article 6 corresponding-adjustment ledgers, F-gas/refrigerant chain-of-custody, recycled-content mass-balance accounting, SAF/maritime book-and-claim and hourly 24/7 matching. Expected duplication at ClimateChain is very low, maybe 0–2 teams each, unless they arrive bundled under a generic "carbon tracking" label.
- Battery passport, DPP and deposit return each have a few precedents. DPP is explicitly suggested by the organizer, so expect several ClimateChain teams to build a generic DPP. A battery-specific angle (SoH, recycled content, EU Battery Regulation fields) is still thin.
- Methane: one prior satellite-methane project (PLUME) shows the concept exists but is not crowded. A methane alert-to-accountability flow (alert → operator response deadline → on-chain record) appears unbuilt in the galleries checked.
- "Prevent double counting" will appear in many ClimateChain carbon-track pitches because the organizer names it. A differentiated idea needs a concrete mechanism, such as an Article 6 corresponding adjustment, a cross-registry retirement check, or a scope-claim double-claim check, not the generic slogan.

### Gaps
- I could not run per-niche Devpost searches. A 30-query niche scan was blocked by Devpost rate limiting (HTTP 429) and was not retried, to avoid bypassing the Cloudflare bot check. Niche counts rely on the 16-query sample, ETHGlobal title search and web search, so they may undercount older Devpost entries.
- `site:devpost.com` web searches proved unreliable (most returned no Devpost pages), so absence in web search is weak evidence by itself.
- Hydrogen certificates were not checked adequately.

## Q4. Which themes win prizes, and which are common but rarely win?

### Takeaway
At small or university hackathons, generic carbon-credit platforms and footprint-reward apps do pick up "winner" flags, simply because they are numerous. In larger sponsor-run hackathons (Hedera, Chainlink), the climate winners are generic themes made concrete: real data or pilots, protocol-native MRV such as Hedera Guardian, or a specific asset class (EV charging, biogas, RECs backing bonds, product-claim QR verification). P2P energy trading, recycling reward tokens and supply-chain traceability are common in galleries but rarely appear among the sponsor-hackathon winners found.

### Inferences
- Carbon-credit marketplaces win often in absolute terms but at a low per-entry rate, because they are so numerous. When they win at sponsor events, they show deeper protocol integration (Guardian dMRV, oracles), a real dataset or pilot, or a specific asset (EV charging sessions, biogas, a real solar plant).
- P2P solar trading, recycling reward tokens and farm-to-fork traceability look like "common but rarely winning" themes in this evidence. They are easy to prototype, hard to make credible, and judges have seen them repeatedly.
- Winning sponsor projects tend to tie on-chain logic to a specific, verifiable physical event or regulated claim: an EV charge session, a biogas output, REC issuance, or a product certification QR. This supports regulation-anchored niches (CBAM embedded emissions, battery passport recycled content, Article 6 adjustments, refrigerant chain-of-custody, methane alert accountability), provided the demo shows a concrete verification mechanism and real or realistic data.
