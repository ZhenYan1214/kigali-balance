## Q1. Are Şile (İSTAÇ, Istanbul) and Menemen (İzmir) in the 2025 UCLA/Carbon Mapper top-25 landfill ranking?

### Takeaway
Yes, both are confirmed in the final list (published 20 Apr 2026): #24 Şile, Türkiye (3.8 t CH4/h, İSTAÇ) and #25 Menemen, Türkiye (3.6 t/h, Metropolitan Municipality of İzmir). Silivri (İSTAÇ's Seymen facility) is a separate "Dishonorable Mention" at 8.4 t/h, higher than every top-25 site. Note that an earlier interim list (Nov 2025) had different ranks, and some secondary write-ups mix the two versions.

### Cited Findings

The "potentially responsible operator" column is UCLA's own research, not Carbon Mapper data — [UCLA STOP Methane, 20 Apr 2026](https://law.ucla.edu/news/spotlight-top-25-methane-plumes-2025-landfills)

UCLA matched İSTAÇ's published GPS coordinates for Silivri-Seymen to the plume, and the site includes an energy production facility — [UCLA, 20 Apr 2026](https://law.ucla.edu/news/spotlight-top-25-methane-plumes-2025-landfills)

## Q2. Turkish LFG power plants: do they hold carbon credits AND YEK-G/I-REC? Is double claiming documented or structurally possible? What do the rules say?

### Takeaway
Turkish LFG projects are widely registered for voluntary credits: Gold Standard for İSTAÇ-area Odayeri/Kömürcüoda, and several VCS projects including an "İzmir LFG Capture and Utilization Project". Turkish rules already block the same facility from issuing both YEK-G and I-REC. I found **no documented case** of a Turkish LFG plant double claiming across a carbon credit and an energy certificate. Structurally, overlap is possible only for the **grid-electricity-displacement** part of a credit. Methane destruction, the bulk of LFG credit value, is a different attribute from the "renewable MWh" in a YEK-G/I-REC. The newly legislated Turkish Carbon Offset System (TR KDS, "Turkuaz" credits) and the Türkiye ETS create a fresh 2026+ risk that legacy GS/VCS projects get claimed twice.

### Cited Findings
- Kömürcüoda + Odayeri LFG project (Istanbul) is listed in the Gold Standard database (PDD 2017, monitoring report Dec 2024). Project participant is Yeşil Global Enerji A.Ş. (formerly Ortadoğu Enerji). It has 38 gas engines: 33.807 MW at Odayeri and 16.980 MW at Kömürcüoda, with generation since 30 Dec 2008 (Odayeri) and 16 Jul 2009 (Kömürcüoda). Surplus gas goes to an enclosed flare — summarized via search from [carbonfootprint.com GS project page](https://www.carbonfootprint.com/gs_turkey_lfg_707.html) and [GMI Kömürcüoda poster](https://www.globalmethane.org/expo-docs/posters/MSW/MSW_TR_Success_Komurcuoda-Landfill_FINAL.pdf). These are secondary or promotional sources; I did not open the GS registry entry.
- Verra lists multiple Turkish LFG projects (Düzce, DNZ, Kayseri, Malatya 2, İzmir, Sinop), including "İzmir Landfill Gas (LFG) Capture and Utilization Project" (public comment page dated 25 Mar 2022; no further details on the page) — [Verra public comment: İzmir LFG](https://verra.org/projects_public_comm/zmir-landfill-gas-lfg-capture-and-utilization-project); [Verra: Düzce LFG](https://verra.org/projects_public_comm/duzce-landfill-gas-lfg-capture-and-utilization-project)
- ITC (Ankara Mamak) is at least 17 MW of LFG per GEM. ITC was acquired by RePie Portföy (Sept 2026). I found no VCS/GS/I-REC linkage for Mamak — [GEM wiki Mamak](https://www.gem.wiki/Mamak_power_station); [Signalbase](https://www.trysignalbase.com/news/acquisitions/itc-invest-trading-consulting-ag-trkiye-acquired-by-repie-portfy-acquisition)
- Turkish LFG capacity figures conflict: 129.4 MW across 16 facilities ([ITU](https://polen.itu.edu.tr/items/8b3bb815-d15e-4780-8bab-525f2e1b0405)), about 180 MW ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/amp/electricity-demand-of-1-million-households-can-be-met-from-landfill-gas-in-turkey-93219)), and more than 250 MW (about 2019, [IAEE abstract](https://iaee.org/en/publications/proceedingsabstractpdf.aspx?id=17426)). None is a verified 2025 figure.
- YEK-G (run by EPİAŞ, supervised by EPDK) covers biomass gas "including landfill gas" (çöp gazı dâhil). A licensee with an active I-REC (or other certificate) registration for the same facility cannot join the YEK-G market. EPİAŞ's position: projects may use different standards "but not for the same MWh" — [Çakmak law firm summary](https://cakmak.av.tr/en/article/renewable-energy-certificates); [EPİAŞ YEK-G presentation](https://www.epias.com.tr/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/ETD-Sunum_Yenilenebilir-Enerji-SertifikalariEPIAS-YEK-G-Sistemi.pdf) (the PDF did not load directly; quote via search summary). YEK-G certificates expire 12 months after the production period — [same](https://cakmak.av.tr/en/article/renewable-energy-certificates)
- Türkiye I-REC demand grew from 0.6 TWh (2020) to 15.3 TWh (2025), per a market vendor document — [Xpansiv Türkiye I-REC Market](https://www.xpansiv.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Turkiye-I-REC-Market-1.2.pdf)
- I-REC lists landfill gas as eligible. The I-REC Secretariat applies country-specific restrictions to prevent double counting (e.g., Mexico requires proof of CEL cancellation since Nov 2022) — [3Degrees](https://3degreesinc.com/insights/exploring-i-recs/); [I-REC Mexico update](https://www.trackingstandard.org/updates-to-the-i-rec-mexico-country-report/)
- Critique: REC/GO verification (Green-e, I-REC) mainly confirms that two RECs are not registered for one MWh — [Offsetguide](https://www.offsetguide.org/?p=2180)
- Gold Standard's published double-counting guideline (page updated 27 Sep 2023) addresses overlap with emissions-trading schemes and host-country accounting, not RECs — [Gold Standard](https://www.goldstandard.org/blog-item/our-new-double-counting-guidelines). A third-party quality assessment flags "landfill gas utilization" as having potential indirect overlaps, but not "landfill gas flaring" — [carboncreditquality.org GS assessment](https://www.carboncreditquality.org/download/Assessments/2.4.3%20GS%20%2831%20May%202022%29.pdf)
- Verra VCS Standard v4.x has section 3.24 "Double Claiming, Other Forms of Credit, and Scope 3 Emissions". I did not retrieve its exact text on RECs — [VCS Standard v4.6](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-standard-v4-6)
- Türkiye Climate Law No. 7552 (2 Jul 2025) creates the TR ETS, with a pilot covering 2026–2027 for CBAM sectors including electricity generation, and the TR KDS offset program issuing "Turkuaz Kredi" for projects outside the ETS that are usable for ETS offsetting — [Para Dergi, Jan 2026](https://www.paradergi.com.tr/sektorler/2026/01/26/emisyon-ticaret-sistemi-icin-geri-sayim-basladi); [EY Türkiye](https://www.ey.com/tr_tr/insights/climate-change-sustainability-services/iklim-kanunu)

### Inferences
- **This kills the naive pitch** ("the same tonne is sold as a carbon credit and as a YEK-G"). A YEK-G represents a MWh of renewable electricity. An LFG carbon credit is mostly tCO2e of destroyed methane. Claiming both is not automatically double counting. The overlap is real only where the credit methodology also credits grid displacement (ACM0001-type "electricity generation" baseline component), and the electricity is also sold as "zero-emission" via YEK-G/I-REC to a buyer who reports Scope 2 reductions. This is a narrower, more defensible claim.
- REC-vs-REC double issuance (YEK-G vs I-REC) is already handled by Turkish rules. Don't pitch that as the gap.
- The live, novel risk surface for 2026: (a) legacy GS/VCS LFG projects re-registering or transitioning into TR KDS (Turkuaz) without cancellation; (b) the same site's reductions counted toward municipal Zero Waste claims, the national inventory/NDC, and voluntary credits sold abroad without corresponding adjustment; (c) municipal "avoided emission" statements (e.g., İSTAÇ's 7,000 t/yr membrane claim) with no conservation check against credits.

### Gaps
- I found no Gold Standard or Verra text that explicitly permits or prohibits RECs or I-RECs for the same MWh as LFG credits. The VCS Standard and GS Principles & Requirements need a direct read.
- I could not determine whether Kömürcüoda/Odayeri electricity is also issued YEK-G or I-REC (the EPİAŞ YEK-G registry and I-REC registry need a check), or whether these plants were under YEKDEM.
- Whether the Kömürcüoda/Odayeri GS crediting includes the grid-displacement component is unknown (the methodology was not seen).
- I did not verify TR KDS secondary legislation on transitioning international voluntary projects.

## Q3. Evidence of landfill-gas over-crediting or under-reporting (reported vs measured)

### Takeaway
There is strong evidence that **reported/modeled landfill emissions** (inventories, GHGRP) are too low: 1.4× (US airborne, Science 2024), about 6× at four SE-US landfills (TROPOMI, ERL 2025), and about 4× for gas-collecting facilities using the "recovery-first" method (Science Advances). Evidence for **credit over-crediting** is weaker and mostly about additionality and baselines, not about the quantity of methane destroyed, which is metered.

### Cited Findings
- Science (28 Mar 2024, Carbon Mapper/NASA JPL/ASU/EPA et al.): airborne surveys of hundreds of US landfills found measured emission rates averaged **1.4×** GHGRP-reported values. Plumes were detected at 52% of landfills, and 60% of large emitters had leaks persisting months to years — [ASU News](https://news.asu.edu/20240329-environment-and-sustainability-new-study-finds-previously-unreported-and-persistent); [WTTW/AP](https://news.wttw.com/2024/03/29/potent-planet-warming-gas-seeping-out-us-landfills-rates-higher-previously-thought)
- Balasus et al., Environmental Research Letters (Jan 2025): TROPOMI 2019–2023 for four SE-US landfills gives emissions on average **6×** the GHGRP-reported values. Satellites show a rising trend while reports show a decrease. All four sites used the "recovery-first" reporting method, which yields about ¼ of the generation-first model. The 19% GHGI decline (2005–2022) reflects a reporting-method shift, not real reductions — [arXiv 2408.10957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10957); [Harvard Salata brief](https://salatainstitute.harvard.edu/research-brief-satellite-monitoring-of-annual-u-s-landfill-methane-emissions/)
- Wang, Jacob, Balasus et al. (12 US urban areas): GHGRP emissions from facilities with gas collection are too low by a factor of **~4** under recovery-first reporting (later in Science Advances) — [arXiv 2505.10835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10835)
- Collection-efficiency defaults (often 75%) are "not based on test data"; proposed range 50–99% by site type — [EREF 2025 executive summary](https://erefdn.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Landfill-Gas-Collection-Efficiency-Executive-Summary.pdf)
- Credit-quality disputes are about additionality: CarbonPlan (Jul 2024) criticized the first CCP-approved CAR landfill credits, and the Climate Action Reserve rebutted — [CAR response to CarbonPlan](https://climateactionreserve.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/CarbonPlan-US-landfill-study-response-09243.pdf). Berkeley Carbon Trading Project (Haya) has an ongoing landfill-gas offset quality analysis (Spring 2025, findings not published in the source) — [Berkeley URAP](https://urapprojects.berkeley.edu/detail.php?id=20208-2)
- California (Nov 2025) amended its Landfill Methane Regulation to require operators to respond to remotely detected plumes, and Colorado followed — [Waste Advantage](https://wasteadvantagemag.com/from-surface-scans-to-satellites-mitigating-legal-risk-as-states-rewrite-landfill-methane-regulations/)

### Inferences
- **Key adversarial point for Idea 1:** a plume over a credited landfill does **not** prove the credits are wrong. Credits count metered destroyed methane; a landfill can destroy X t (credited correctly) and still leak Y t. What satellites contradict is (a) **reported/inventory emissions** (the NDC and national inventory), (b) **modeled "avoided" claims** such as composting/diversion credits built on first-order-decay baselines, membrane-cover "prevented" claims, and Zero Waste methane-avoidance numbers, and (c) **implied collection efficiencies** used in reporting. An honest AI layer should flag "implied collection efficiency" and "inventory-vs-observed gap", not "fake credits".
- Satellite snapshots (instantaneous t/h, daylight and clear-sky only) are not annual totals. UCLA itself warns about this (Q1). Any fusion model must output intervals, not point verdicts.

### Gaps
- No Türkiye-specific study comparing reported landfill emissions or collection efficiency to satellite measurements was found.
- I did not locate RMI/CATF/Industrious Labs quantitative over-crediting studies. The Industrious Labs search returned nothing relevant.

## Q4. Existing solutions and prior art (what already kills or overlaps the idea)

### Takeaway
The **observation + model fusion** half of Idea 1 is already built: RMI/CATF/Global Methane Hub **WasteMAP** aggregates reported, modeled and observed waste methane with a decision-support tool. Carbon Mapper, UCLA and IMEO provide plumes and operator attribution. Blockchain MRV for LFG credits already exists in **Hedera Guardian** (ACR, CAR and CDM ACM0001 LFG policies). I found no tool that reconciles **claims across claim classes** (voluntary credit / energy certificate / municipal / national) per site-period. That is the only surviving novelty.

### Cited Findings
- WasteMAP (launched in beta at COP28, Dec 2023; Global Methane Hub with RMI, CATF, SRON/GHGSat, Carbon Mapper; built by Earth Genome) is an "open, online platform that aggregates and maps reported, modeled, and observed waste methane emissions data", with a decision-support tool to set baselines and model scenarios. It had more than 3,000 disposal-site plumes as of end-June 2025 — [RMI WasteMAP](https://rmi.org/introducing-wastemap-a-new-tool-to-track-and-reduce-waste-methane-emissions); [RMI WasteMAP insight](https://rmi.org/insight/waste-methane-assessment-platform); [Earth Genome](https://www.earthgenome.org/work/102---wastemap)
- Hedera Guardian has open policy templates for ACR LFG v2.0, CAR US Landfill Protocol v6.0 and CDM ACM0001, minting tokens tied to MRV documents (verifiable credentials, IPFS + Hedera Consensus Service) — [Guardian ACR LFG](https://guardian.hedera.com/guardian/demo-guide/carbon-offsets/landfill-gas-destruction-and-beneficial-use-projects-version-2.0); [Guardian ACM0001](https://guardian.hedera.com/docs/3.6.0/guardian/demo-guide/carbon-offsets/cdm-acm0001-flaring-or-use-of-landfill-gas); [IPFS blog 2022](https://blog.ipfs.tech/2022-11-10-guardian-ipfs-and-hedera/)
- CATF + GHGSat monitored 13 African landfills and shared findings with stakeholders — [ISWA guest blog](https://www.iswa.org/?p=100365)
- ESA-funded Landfill Methane Monitor and the Leicester/MEDUSA project assess "whether repairs have worked" (Spain case study) — [ESA LMM](https://business.esa.int/projects/landfill-methane-monitor-lmm); [Leicester, Dec 2025](https://le.ac.uk/news/2025/december/satellites-landfill-methane-leaks)
- I found no Devpost/hackathon project combining landfill methane, satellites and a claims ledger. The nearest crypto item is Marathon's Bitcoin mining on landfill gas (unrelated) — [MARA](https://mara.com/posts/cashing-in-on-trash-bitcoin-mining-fixes-landfill-emissions)

### Inferences
- If Idea 1 is pitched as "AI fuses satellite + model to flag bad landfills", judges aware of WasteMAP will see it as a clone. It has to be pitched as a **claims-conservation layer on top of WasteMAP/Carbon Mapper data**.
- Guardian proves a blockchain LFG credit pipeline is feasible but covers **one registry's issuance**, not conservation across registries and claim types.

### Gaps
- I did not verify in this session whether CAD Trust (Climate Action Data Trust) covers voluntary registries' LFG projects or any energy-certificate registries. My understanding is that it covers carbon credits only (not EACs), but this is **unverified**.
- I did not check Climate TRACE's waste-sector coverage of Turkish sites.

## Q6. Idea 1 verdict: Waste-methane claim reconciliation

### Takeaway
**Wounded but survives in narrowed form.** Two attacks succeed. Satellite plumes don't invalidate metered destruction credits, and observation/model fusion already exists (WasteMAP). The surviving gap is a **site-period claims-conservation registry across claim classes** (voluntary credit [GS/VCS], new TR KDS Turkuaz credit, YEK-G/I-REC electricity attribute, municipal Zero Waste claims, national inventory), with satellite-derived "implied collection efficiency" as a plausibility signal. It is timely because TR KDS/ETS launch in 2026 and Türkiye has 2–3 super-emitters at COP31.

### Cited Findings
- Supporting facts: see Q1 (Şile #24, Menemen #25, Silivri DM), Q2 (GS Kömürcüoda/Odayeri; VCS İzmir LFG; YEK-G/I-REC exclusivity; TR KDS), Q3 (inventory undercount 1.4–6×), Q4 (WasteMAP, Guardian) — sources as cited above.

### Inferences
- **One-sentence problem:** Starting in 2026, the same Turkish landfill's methane can be claimed at once as an international voluntary credit, a domestic Turkuaz credit, a renewable-electricity certificate, a municipal Zero Waste achievement and a national NDC reduction. No system checks that these claims add up, or that they match what satellites see. Meanwhile three Turkish sites rank among the world's worst landfill emitters.
- **Primary user:** the İklim Değişikliği Başkanlığı (Climate Change Presidency), as TR KDS registry operator and inventory compiler, and the verifiers (VVBs) approving TR KDS transitions. Secondary users: EAC buyers and corporate Scope 2/3 auditors, and municipalities (İSTAÇ, İzmir) wanting credible Zero Waste claims.
- **Minimal compelling demo:** take a single site, "Şile/Kömürcüoda 2025". (1) Ingest the public Carbon Mapper plume rates for that site (3.8 t/h average). (2) Ingest the GS project's monitored destroyed CH4 (from its monitoring report, or a mocked figure if unavailable, labeled as such) and the LFG MWh. (3) Compute implied generation (FOD model from waste-in-place) vs destroyed vs observed leak, giving an implied collection efficiency with an uncertainty band. (4) The claims ledger shows four claim tokens against the site-period: GS VER (methane destruction), YEK-G (MWh), a TR KDS application (attempted), and a municipal "7,000 t avoided" statement. A conservation rule rejects the TR KDS issuance that overlaps a GS vintage not cancelled. A flag marks the municipal avoided-emission claim as inconsistent with persistent plumes. Under 3 minutes, with real Turkish site names and real public numbers.
- **Hardest judge objections and honest answers:**
  1. "Satellites see leaks; credits count destroyed gas. You're comparing apples and oranges." Answer: correct, and the design reflects it. We never invalidate destruction credits from plumes. We flag the *other* claims (inventory, avoided-emission, Zero Waste methane benefit) and compute implied collection efficiency, which matters for baseline and inventory credibility.
  2. "Is there any evidence of actual double claiming in Türkiye?" Answer: none documented (we checked). YEK-G already excludes I-REC for the same facility. The risk is prospective: TR KDS/ETS from 2026, plus legacy GS/VCS projects, plus municipal and NDC claims. Conservation checks are cheap before the market launches and very expensive after a scandal. Don't overclaim.
  3. "Why not WasteMAP plus a SQL table at the Climate Presidency?" Answer: WasteMAP doesn't track claims. A single ministry database can't bind *foreign* registries (GS/Verra/I-REC) and municipalities that don't trust each other's books. A shared append-only ledger where each registry posts claim commitments (hashes of serials plus site-period) gives cross-registry uniqueness checks without anyone handing over its database. Honest caveat: CAD Trust already does this for carbon credits across registries, so our delta is adding the EAC + municipal + inventory claim classes.
- **Is blockchain genuinely necessary?** Partially. A multi-party, no-single-owner conservation check across independent registries (GS, Verra, TR KDS, EPİAŞ YEK-G, I-REC, municipalities) is one of the few legitimate blockchain use cases, which is CAD Trust's own rationale. A centralized database run by the Climate Presidency would work technically but fails the trust test for foreign registries. Keep on-chain data minimal: claim commitments plus a uniqueness rule, with evidence off-chain.

### Gaps
- No real per-MWh linkage between a GS LFG project and YEK-G issuance was confirmed. The demo must label that component as a hypothetical scenario unless registry data is found.

## Q7. Does IMEO/MARS or Carbon Mapper already publish per-alert tracking and follow-up? Is there an NGO tracker?

### Takeaway
Partially. IMEO publishes plume detections (with a delay of 30, or 45–75 days in the beta era), a filter showing whether an event was notified, **country-level** response rates and a count of verified mitigations. I found **no public per-alert record of operator responses, claimed fixes and re-check outcomes**. Carbon Mapper publishes repeat observations (persistence) but no operator-response field. ## Q8. Idea 2 verdict: Methane alert accountability ledger

### Takeaway
**Mostly killed as a blockchain project; survives as a narrower, Türkiye-focused public accountability tracker.** IMEO already tracks responses (privately per alert, publicly per country), and IEA/IMEO published a Response Blueprint for exactly this workflow. Blockchain adds little over an open, timestamped database. The best residual angle is a "COP31 accountability clock" for the three Turkish waste sites, with operator statements and satellite re-checks timestamped.

### Cited Findings
- See Q7 (MARS response tracking, Response Blueprint, waste expansion May 2026, publication delay) and Q1 (İSTAÇ statement that uncapped areas may be the cause; ministry did not respond).

### Inferences
- **One-sentence problem:** When satellites publicly show a landfill super-emitting, nobody keeps a public, tamper-evident record of what the operator then promised, whether it acted, and whether later satellite passes confirm the fix. Promises like İSTAÇ's "improvements are needed" disappear after the news cycle.
- **Primary user:** journalists, NGOs and municipal councils (e.g., the İBB and İzmir city councils), plus COP31 observers. Secondary: IMEO as an external validation channel.
- **Minimal compelling demo:** a timeline for Silivri, Şile and Menemen. Plume observations from the Carbon Mapper portal, then an operator statement (Guardian/Evrensel quote, hashed and timestamped), then a "claimed fix" (membrane cover, clay capping), then a satellite re-observation rate after the claimed date, ending in a status of "unverified / improving / persistent".
- **Hardest judge objections and honest answers:**
  1. "IMEO already does this." Answer: IMEO tracks responses per alert internally but publishes only country-level rates and a mitigation count. Alerts go to governments first and are not public per alert. We cover only public detections and add operator-level timelines. This is a complement, not a replacement.
  2. "Why blockchain? A GitHub repo with signed commits does this." Answer: honestly, mostly true. The only blockchain-specific value is a neutral timestamp that operators can't later dispute or quietly edit, which OpenTimestamps or a single hash anchor provides. A full on-chain ledger isn't justified.
  3. "Satellite revisits are sparse and cloud-limited; a 'fix verified' status is statistically weak." Answer: correct, which UCLA itself acknowledges. Output detection-probability-aware statuses (e.g., "no plume in N clear passes, detection limit ~X kg/h") rather than binary "fixed".
- **Is blockchain genuinely necessary?** No. Hash-anchoring is enough. As a hackathon entry it is weaker than Idea 1 on the "blockchain + AI" criterion. It could be absorbed into Idea 1 as the "evidence timeline" module.

- I did not check whether Carbon Mapper's portal provides an API field for observation counts without detection (needed for "no plume in N passes").
