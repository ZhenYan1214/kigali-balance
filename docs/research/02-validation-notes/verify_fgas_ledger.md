# Adversarial verification: HFC refrigerant (F-gas) lifecycle ledger for IEEE ClimateChain Hackathon 2026

Research date: 2026-10-14. Scope: try to kill the idea (existing systems, regulations that make blockchain unnecessary, overstated pain), then define the surviving gap and the strongest framing. Note: several primary PDFs (JRECO RaMS deck, EIA 2026 banks report, EIA 2024 carbon-credit briefing) were binary/compressed and could not be text-extracted; where that matters it is listed under Gaps.

## Q1. Does the EU system (F-gas Portal + licensing + customs Single Window + record-keeping + national registries) already do this?

### Takeaway
Only in pieces. The EU F-gas Portal plus CERTEX checks importer registration/licence and bulk-HFC quota at the border. Downstream records are kept, but in national silos with different formats. Italy has a mandatory electronic database of technician interventions, and France has a mandatory digital tracking slip for recovered refrigerant waste. Yet Italy is still the EU's biggest illegal-HFC hotspot. That is the strongest evidence that siloed digital records do not stop fraud when nobody reconciles mass across segments.

### Cited Findings
- Reg. (EU) 2024/573 sets up an electronic system (the F-gas Portal) for quota management, import/export licensing and reporting. Implementing Reg. (EU) 2024/2473 governs registration. — [EUR-Lex 2024/2473](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2024/2473/oj/eng/pdf); [lexaris ToC of 2024/573](https://lexaris.de/de/library/tableofcontents/4646105)
- Implementing Reg. 2024/2473 integrates the F-gas Portal with the EU Customs Single Window (CERTEX) so customs can carry out the 2024/573 controls. Commission enforcement guidelines (v1.0, 30 June 2025) say an active F-gas Portal registration at the moment of import, transit or export counts as the licence. Quota/authorisation applies to release for free circulation of bulk HFCs and to HFCs in RAC equipment, heat pumps and MDIs. HFCs may be placed on the market only to the extent the importer holds enough quota at that moment. — [Luxembourg customs: CERTEX enforcement guidelines](https://douanes.public.lu/content/dam/douanes/fr/prohibitions-restrictions/certex/certex-enforcement-fgas-regulation-eo-guidelines.pdf); [refindustry on 2024/2473](https://refindustry.com/news/amendment-to-the-rules-of-the-f-gas-portal-new-implementing-regulation-eu-2024-2473/)
- An Irish Revenue notice (12 March 2026) says the next CERTEX phase, scheduled for May 2026, covers electronic validation for export of ODS and F-gases, including a check that the F-gas registration number fits the products, procedure and exporter. — [Revenue eCustoms notification 05/2026](https://www.revenue.ie/en/customs/businesses/electronic-systems/ais/ecustoms-notifications/2026/ecustoms-notification-05-2026.pdf)
- Under 2024/573, record-keeping is **Article 7, not Article 6**. Article 6 now covers leak detection systems. Operators of equipment subject to leak checks under Art. 5(1) must keep records per piece of equipment, and suppliers must keep records on who buys F-gases (Art. 7(3)). — [dip.gov.cz obligations map](https://dip.gov.cz/dip/en/query/4/2024/573); [Castel summary of 2024 F-gas Reg](https://castel.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024_NEW_F_GAS_Regulation_Castel.pdf)
- EU-level record keeping has no mandatory EU electronic logbook. Industry offers voluntary tools such as the EFCTC electronic logbook and AREA's spreadsheet. — [EFCTC logbook](https://fluorocarbons.org/newsletter/efctc-newsletter-issue-n163-december-2018-january-2019/?frame=0); [AREA](https://area-eur.be/node/703)
- Reclaimed gas is treated favourably. Labelling (Art. 12) applies from 1 Jan 2025, and reclaimed containers must show the EU address of the reclamation facility (per a commercial summary of Art. 12(7)). The servicing ban for GWP ≥ 2500 in large refrigeration is postponed to 1 Jan 2030 for reclaimed gas labelled under Art. 12. — [General Gas blog](https://pay.generalgas.it/blog/placing-reclaimed-refrigerant-gases-on-the-market-regulatory-prerequisites.htm); [Castel](https://castel.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024_NEW_F_GAS_Regulation_Castel.pdf)
- **Italy, national electronic registry:** since 25 Sept 2019, record-keeping is met by reporting every installation, maintenance, repair, leak check and dismantling on stationary RAC/heat pumps, fire protection, refrigerated trucks and switchgear to the **Banca Dati F-gas** (DPR 146/2018) within 30 days. A professional site cites fines of €1,000–15,000 for missing or late reports (single source). — [CCIAA Firenze slides](https://www.fi.camcom.gov.it/sites/default/files/uploads/Registri_Albi_Certificazioni/Ambiente/Convegni_e_Seminari/Interventi_Fgas_FOCUS.pdf); [studiomadera 2026](https://studiomadera.it/news/808-fgas); [certifico manual](https://www.certifico.com/impianti/news-impianti/banca-dati-f-gas-richiesta-abilitazioni-comunicazione-interventi-manuale-utente)
- **France, mandatory digital tracking of recovered refrigerant:** the BSFF (bordereau de suivi des fluides frigorigènes) must be done on the state platform **Trackdéchets** from 1 Jan 2023 (arrêté of 26 July 2022). Paper CERFA 15497 was tolerated only until 31 March 2023. Installers/maintainers, transporters and treatment operators must all be registered. — [FFB](https://www.ffbatiment.fr/actualites-batiment/actualite-ba/suivi-des-dechets-dangereux-utiliser-trackdechets-est-desormais-obligatoire); [CAPEB](https://www.capeb.fr/actualites/trackdechets-un-nouvel-outil-obligatoire-pour-les-producteurs-de-dechets-dangereux-amiante-fluides-frigorigenes...); [DREETS ARA](https://auvergne-rhone-alpes.dreets.gouv.fr/sites/auvergne-rhone-alpes.dreets.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/02_trackdechets.pdf)
- **Netherlands:** there is a searchable central register of certified persons and companies (Centraal Register Techniek, formerly STEK-linked). Leak-check and repair info must be kept in a logbook for 5 years. — [Cooling Post](https://www.coolingpost.com/world-news/netherlands-has-searchable-f-gas-register/); [business.gov.nl](https://business.gov.nl/regulation/certificates-working-with-f-gases/)
- A dated (2015) AREA survey reported operative, accessible F-gas databases in Denmark, France, Italy, Portugal, Slovakia, Spain and Sweden. In Denmark, all F-gas companies and engineers are registered with Kølebranchen. — [Cooling Post 2015](https://www.coolingpost.com/world-news/netherlands-has-searchable-f-gas-register/)
- **Fraud persists despite these systems:** reported EU HFC seizures rose from about 250 t (2023) to about 670 t (2024), roughly 2.7x, matching "nearly tripled". Italy reported nearly 500 t seized in 2024 and is the member state with the highest seizure volume. Fraud modes include mislabelling virgin high-GWP HFC as reclaimed, exceeding quota, falsifying invoices, VAT evasion and counterfeit lower-GWP blends. HFC prices have risen about tenfold since the phase-down began. Italy set up a permanent F-gas roundtable (environment ministry, customs, Carabinieri) in Jan 2026. — [refindustry on EIA report](https://refindustry.com/news/eia-report-tracks-illegal-hfc-trade-into-eu-supply-chains/); [EIA press release](https://eia-international.org/news/organised-crime-allegedly-supplies-illegal-refrigerants-to-big-italian-supply-chains)
- EIA's "Fakes, Fraud and F-Gases" (April 2026) quotes EIA's Fin Walravens: industry associations claim nearly half of the refrigerant gas on the Italian market is illegal. He adds that estimating the scale is difficult. A 50% EU supply cut is due in 2027. — [ACR Journal](https://www.acrjournal.uk/news/organised-crime-allegedly-supplies-illegal-refrigerants-to-major-itali); [EIA report PDF](https://eia-international.org/wp-content/uploads/2026-EIA-Fakes-Frauds-and-F-Gases-SPREADS.pdf)

### Inferences
- The EU **does** cover the import-to-first-placing-on-market link: registration, licence, quota at customs, and annual reporting. Building a "quota ledger" for importers would duplicate the F-gas Portal, so that part of the idea is dead in the EU.
- What the EU does **not** have is one system that reconciles kilograms across distributor sales → technician charges → recovery → reclaim → destruction. National systems each cover one segment: Italy covers interventions, France covers recovered waste movements, NL/DK cover certification. They are not linked to quota-backed supply. Mislabelling virgin gas as "reclaimed" sits exactly in that seam. Reclaimed gas is quota-exempt and escapes the 2030 servicing ban, so the label is valuable, and nothing reconciles reclaimer output against documented recovered input.
- Italy is the key test case. It has the EU Portal plus a mandatory 30-day electronic intervention database, and is still the worst hotspot. More digital silos are therefore not the answer. The surviving gap is **cross-segment mass-balance reconciliation**, not "digitise logbooks".
- Within a single EU member state, a central regulator could run that reconciliation in an ordinary database. Blockchain is hardest to justify there.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from primary text whether CERTEX does a live quota-balance decrement per shipment or only a registration/licence validity check. The guideline text suggests quota must be held "at that moment", but the mechanism was not confirmed.
- No current (2023–2026) detail on Danish, Swedish, German or Belgian logbook systems. The only source is dated 2015.
- Could not find evidence on whether Italian Banca Dati entries are cross-checked against supplier sales or quota data. The inference that they are not reconciled is plausible but unverified.

## Q2. Türkiye: FARAVET, EKOMVET, technician certification, illegal trade, Kigali

### Takeaway
Türkiye already mirrors the EU two-silo design. FARAVET covers quota, import/export control documents and annual activity reports. EKOMVET covers equipment-operator registration and intervention records. Türkiye is also a documented **source** of illegal HFCs smuggled into the EU via Bulgaria. This makes Türkiye a strong COP31-host story, but the pitch must be "connect FARAVET ↔ EKOMVET ↔ end-of-life", not "digitise from scratch".

### Cited Findings
- The F-gas regulation ("Florlu Sera Gazlarına İlişkin Yönetmelik") was published in Resmî Gazete on **15 Oct 2024, issue 32693**, and entered into force the same day. It repeals the 29.06.2022 regulation (issue 31881). Its legal bases include the Environment Law and the Kigali Amendment. — [alomaliye (RG text)](https://www.alomaliye.com/2024/10/15/florlu-sera-gazlarina-iliskin-yonetmelik/); [Erdem & Erdem](https://www.erdem-erdem.av.tr/bilgi-bankasi/florlu-sera-gazlarina-iliskin-yonetmelik); [KPMG Vergi](https://kpmgvergi.com/yayinlar/mali-bultenler/gumruk/florlu-sera-gazlarina-iliskin-yonetmelik-yayimlanmistir/2829)
- **FARAVET:** anyone who imports, exports or places F-gases on the market (bulk or in products), uses them in manufacturing, or recovers, recycles, reclaims or destroys them must register. FARAVET handles annual activity reports, quota applications and tracking, and HFC Control Documents for import/export. Since 2024, HFC imports have been executed through FARAVET against allocated quotas. Ministry decks give deadlines of 31 May (quota applications), 30 Sept (quota announcements) and 31 March (prior-year reporting). Established firms that do not report by the deadline cannot apply for quota. — [CSB ozone unit decks](https://webdosya.csb.gov.tr/db/ozonturkiye/haberler/1.-florlu-sera-gazlarina-il-sk-n-yonetmel-k-20250627154853.pdf); [CSB 2025 deck](https://webdosya.csb.gov.tr/v2/ozonturkiye/2025/12/Ankara_Y-netmelik_GBA-20251231165232.pdf)
- **EKOMVET:** registration is mandatory for operators of stationary RAC/heat pumps, fire protection, refrigerated trucks/trailers and ORC equipment with ≥5 tCO2e F-gas, and for switchgear with ≥6 kg SF6. Records cover equipment plus installation, maintenance, leak checks and refills. Leak-check periods are tracked in EKOMVET. Service firms need a TSE Hizmet Yeri Belgesi and technicians need MYK national qualification certificates. — [CSB Istanbul service deck 2026](https://webdosya.csb.gov.tr/v2/ozonturkiye/2026/02/stanbul_Servis_Y-netmelik_AON-20260213135940.pdf); [CSB MYO deck 2026](https://webdosya.csb.gov.tr/v2/ozonturkiye/2026/06/MYO_-al-tay_-EB-20260622164751.pdf)
- Türkiye bans disposable (non-refillable) containers for servicing/filling and bans unlabelled F-gas containers/equipment on the market. — [CSB May 2024 deck](https://webdosya.csb.gov.tr/db/ozonturkiye/haberler/2_florlu-sera-gazlarina-il-sk-n-yonetmel-k-ve-p-yasaya-arz-yasaklari-20240515143923.pdf)
- A **draft circular on management of waste F-gases** ("Taslak Atık Florlu Sera Gazlarının Yönetimine İlişkin Genelge") exists on iklim.gov.tr, so end-of-life rules are still being built. — [iklim.gov.tr draft](https://iklim.gov.tr/db/turkce/icerikler/files/TASLAK%20ATIK%20FLORLU%20SERA%20GAZLARININ%20YONETIMINE%20ILISKIN%20GENELGE.pdf)
- Türkiye's ministry runs F-gas customs training (Feb 2026) presenting the EU-style rule that the Commission issues import/export licences via the F-gas Portal. This is relevant to EU-bound trade. — [CSB customs training](https://webdosya.csb.gov.tr/v2/ozonturkiye/2026/02/2026_02_03_Customs-training_F-gases_TR_JM-1-20260213144508.pdf)
- **Illegal trade:** EIA reports illegal HFCs sourced mainly from Türkiye and China, routed through Bulgaria (a land border with known corruption risk) to Greece, Germany, France, Italy, Portugal and Spain. Smugglers now avoid banned disposable cylinders and disguise HFCs as HFO-1234yf. — [EIA "More Chilling Than Ever"](https://eia-international.org/report/more-chilling-than-ever/)
- Bulgarian seizures at the Turkish border include 490 kg of R134a/R404A in 40 illegal non-refillable cylinders at Kapitan Andreevo, and 96 cylinders (1,123.2 kg) in a Turkish-owned truck. Bulgaria's Anti-Corruption Bureau arrested four members of an organised group importing HFCs illegally. — [Cooling Post](https://coolingpost.com/world-news/bulgarians-seize-500kg-of-illegal-refrigerant); [Cooling Post](https://coolingpost.com/world-news/bulgarian-customs-seize-over-a-tonne-of-illegal-hfcs); [Cooling Post arrests](https://www.coolingpost.com/world-news/bulgarian-hfc-smugglers-arrested/)
- Kigali: Türkiye ratified on 10 Nov 2021 (129th party), with entry into force 8 Feb 2022. — [Cooling Post](https://www.coolingpost.com/world-news/turkey-latest-to-ratify-kigali/); [ozonturkiye](https://ozonturkiye.csb.gov.tr/en/news/turkey-ratified-kigali-amendment-265276)

### Inferences
- Türkiye's import side is already digitised and quota-gated in FARAVET. The equipment side, EKOMVET, records refills, which are the leak signal, and only certified technicians can make entries. The unlinked parts are distributor-level sales between import and technician, and end-of-life, where the waste-F-gas circular is still a draft.
- Because Türkiye is a smuggling source for the EU, a "cross-border provenance" angle is credible: an EU buyer or customs could verify that a cylinder from Türkiye traces to FARAVET-quota-backed import or documented recovery. This is speculative framing, since no source proposes it.

### Gaps
- **Türkiye's Montreal Protocol classification (Article 5 vs non-Article 5) and HFC schedule were NOT confirmed.** Search summaries inferred non-Article 5 from group lists, but this is unverified. Check the UNEP Ozone Secretariat party status list before stating any schedule.
- Exact fields stored in FARAVET and EKOMVET (cylinder serial? kg per intervention? gas batch?) were not confirmed from system documentation. Only ministry slide summaries were available.
- No source found on illegal HFC volumes within Türkiye's domestic market.

## Q3. Taiwan HFC management

### Takeaway
Taiwan's regime is new. It took effect in 2025 and is consumption- and quota-focused, with reporting of "execution performance" by users and suppliers. A recovery/destruction offset methodology was under MOENV review in July 2026. No public evidence was found of a Taiwanese cylinder-level digital tracking platform. Taiwan is a plausible greenfield, but evidence is thin.

### Cited Findings
- 《氫氟碳化物管理辦法》 (HFC Management Regulation) was issued by 環境部 (MOENV) on 2025-02-25 (民國114年). It has 20 articles, took effect on issuance, and rests on 氣候變遷因應法 Art. 38(3). It defines 執行實績 (usage, import/export release, purchase, inventory and sales quantities, with evidence) for users and suppliers, defines 回收 and 回用, and excludes recovery/reuse/destruction from consumption. From 2026-01-01, consumption must not exceed 100% of baseline. — [6laws 氫氟碳化物管理辦法](https://6laws.net/6law/law3/氫氟碳化物管理辦法.htm); [Lee and Li newsletter](https://www.leeandli.com/TW/Newsletters/1724.htm)
- On 22 July 2026, MOENV reviewed a draft methodology for 廢棄含氟氣體回收與銷毀 (recovery and destruction of waste fluorinated gases) so that projects can enter voluntary reduction schemes and earn credits (碳權). Sources cited: waste electrical/electronic and IT products about 70%, vehicles about 20%, semiconductor processes about 10%. A Taiwanese association official stressed that reductions count only if the amount destroyed is quantified. — [PTS 公視](https://news.pts.org.tw/article/818802)
- A 2023 MOENV overseas-mission report studied Singapore's refrigerant recovery and flow-management (流向管理) regime as input to Taiwan's own control measures. — [NDC report](https://report.ndc.gov.tw/ReportFront/PageSystem/reportFileDownload/C11201321/001)
- In 2025 MOENV ran a NT$50M 冷卻行動示範運行補助 (cooling action demonstration subsidy) for low-GWP retrofits. This is equipment replacement, not a recovery incentive. — [工商時報](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20250624701518-430104)

### Inferences
- Taiwan is about to create domestic F-gas destruction credits. That creates a need for chain-of-custody evidence so the same kg is not credited in Taiwan's voluntary scheme and an international registry (Verra/Isometric), and to prove the gas came from waste equipment rather than stock. This is a relevant local hook if the team is Taiwan-based.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of a Taiwanese refrigerant recovery reward (回收獎勵) or a digital refrigerant 流向 platform. Waste refrigerant from appliances may flow under 廢四機 recycling/資源回收管理基金 data, but this was not verified.
- Final status of the July 2026 draft methodology is unknown.

## Q4. Japan: closest existing equivalent (Fluorocarbon Emissions Control Act + JRECO RaMS)

### Takeaway
Japan is the strongest "kill" evidence and also the strongest "survive" evidence. Japan has had a statutory lifecycle regime for decades (since 2001, revised 2019/2020), with an officially designated information-processing centre (JRECO RaMS) that digitises charging/recovery records and the 行程管理票 (process-control manifest). Even so, recovery at disposal is stuck at about 40–44%. In 2024, about 6,878 t of HFC (12.6 MtCO2e) was emitted at disposal. Lifecycle digital paperwork alone has not fixed recovery. Japanese experts are now explicitly proposing "refrigerant credits" and "integrated data management for traceability".

### Cited Findings
- The Fluorocarbon Emissions Control Act covers the whole lifecycle: reduced shipments, inspections, leakage reports, mandatory recovery and destruction at disposal of commercial equipment. Residential ACs fall under the Home Appliance Recycling Law. The 2019 revision is under 5-year review. — [MOEJ OEWG48 side-event deck, 15 July 2026](https://www.jraia.or.jp/english/relations/file/260724_oewg48_sideevent_moej_presentation.pdf)
- **2024 HFC material flow (MOEJ):** stock in operating equipment about 210,000 t (about 300 MtCO2e). Amount remaining at disposal 12,011 t (21.85 MtCO2e). Recovered at disposal 5,188 t (about 43%). **Emission at disposal 6,878 t (12.62 MtCO2e)**. Emissions during use 6,283 t (11.69 MtCO2e). Reclaimed 3,712 t, destroyed 2,869 t. Note the gap between operator-reported leakage (575 t) and inventory-estimated use-phase emissions (6,283 t), a more than 10x under-reporting signal. — [MOEJ deck p.6](https://www.jraia.or.jp/english/relations/file/260724_oewg48_sideevent_moej_presentation.pdf)
- Japan's 2024 HFC emissions were about 27.6 MtCO2e (+25% vs 2013), against targets of about 8.93 Mt in 2030 and 4.47 Mt in 2040. Commercial ACs at disposal are the largest share at 20% (5.5 Mt), and residential ACs at disposal 16% (4.5 Mt). — [MOEJ deck pp.1–2](https://www.jraia.or.jp/english/relations/file/260724_oewg48_sideevent_moej_presentation.pdf)
- Recovery rate at disposal for commercial equipment was 44% in FY2022, "stalled in the 40% range". JRAIA cites an improvement from 40% to 44% after the revised Act. An earlier analysis gave 38% (FY2015), roughly 30% for 10+ years before that. — [JRAIA/OEWG47 deck](https://www.jraia.or.jp/english/relations/file/2507asap_%20OEWG47%20Sideevent_JRAIA%20Ms.%20Yamanaka%20presentation.pdf); [env.go.jp](https://www.env.go.jp/content/000388554.pdf)
- **RaMS:** JRECO operates RaMS as the statutory 情報処理センター under Arts. 76–85 of the Act. It supports electronic issuance, delivery and storage of 行程管理票 in the same format as paper, so paper management is unnecessary. Charging/recovery registrations cost ¥100 per record (excl. tax) per the July 2024 overview. — [JRECO RaMS 概要 2024](https://www.jreco.or.jp/data/rams_gaiyo2024.pdf); [JRECO RaMS 概要 2021](https://www.jreco.or.jp/data/rams_gaiyo2021.pdf); [JRECO PV manual](https://www.jreco.or.jp/data/1_pv_manual.pdf)
- At HVAC&R JAPAN 2026 (30 Jan 2026), RaMS and **RaMS-ex** (digital extension) were presented as improving traceability of charging, recovery, reclamation and destruction. Tokyo reported IoT leak monitoring. Prof. Kagawa proposed **refrigerant credits**, incentives, **integrated data management for traceability**, and exporting Japan's system design to Southeast Asia. — [IIR news, 24 June 2026](https://iifiir.org/en/news/japan-explores-policy-and-digital-tools-for-a-refrigerant-circular-economy)
- The METI/MOE joint council published an interim summary (中間とりまとめ) in Aug 2026 on further fluorocarbon emission measures. — [env.go.jp](https://www.env.go.jp/content/000420605.pdf)
- About 1,700 demolition-site inspections by local governments were made in 2023, rising year on year. — [MOEJ deck p.4](https://www.jraia.or.jp/english/relations/file/260724_oewg48_sideevent_moej_presentation.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Nobody has a lifecycle ledger" is false. Japan has one, statutory and digital. The defensible claim is narrower: **a ledger that only records paperwork does not create recovery**. Japan's bottleneck is the missing economic incentive at the moment of disposal and the gap between reported and actual leakage (575 t vs 6,283 t). A hackathon project should present itself as adding (a) mass-balance anomaly detection on top of RaMS-type records and (b) a value signal (credits/reclaim premium) attached to verified recovered kg. It should not claim to invent lifecycle tracking.
- RaMS is a single-country, centralised, fee-per-record system. Japan wants to export its model to SE Asia, so there is room for an interoperable, multi-jurisdiction provenance layer.

### Gaps
- No RaMS adoption figures (share of 行程管理票 issued electronically vs paper) were found. The JRECO OEWG48 deck could not be text-extracted.
- No causal study found on why the recovery rate is stuck. Candidate causes (cost borne by owner at demolition, enforcement gaps) are not sourced here.

## Q5. Global South, refrigerant banks, destruction credits, integrity and double-claiming

### Takeaway
The climate stakes are large and well sourced. Banks hold about 13–24 GtCO2e. Lifecycle management could avoid about 39 GtCO2e from 2025–2050 (TEAP 2024 via UNEP). Drawdown gives 57.15 GtCO2e from 2020–2050 under a 100%-leak-prevention assumption. Destruction credit methodologies for HFCs in Article 5 countries became available only in late 2025–2026 (Verra VM0016 v2.0, Isometric protocol). Integrity critiques are real (HFC-23 CDM history, EIA's opposition). A documented "double-claiming between destruction credits and Kigali accounting" problem was **not** found in the literature. It is a logical risk, not an evidenced one.

### Cited Findings
- Project Drawdown, Refrigerant Management: 57.15 GtCO2e avoided 2020–2050, assuming 100% prevention of leaks plus proper end-of-life destruction. Net lifetime cost is about US$622.73B (net cost, not saving). Older editions cite 57.7–57.75 Gt. — [drawdown.org](https://drawdown.org/solutions/refrigerant-management)
- EIA (2026): global ODS/HFC banks hold about 13–24 GtCO2e (indicative). Annual bank emissions are about 1.5 GtCO2e (about 3% of global GHG). Current financing is inadequate for collection, recovery and destruction. — [EIA 2026 banks report](https://eia-international.org/wp-content/uploads/2026-EIA-Evaluating-Options-for-Sustainably-Financed-Action-on-ODS-and-HFC-Banks.pdf); [EIA MOP37 side-event](https://ozone.unep.org/system/files/documents/EIA_ODS%20HFC%20banks_side%20event_MOP37.pdf)
- TEAP 2024, as cited in a July 2026 UNEP guide: lifecycle refrigerant management could prevent an additional 39 GtCO2e of HFC+HCFC emissions 2025–2050, up to 67 Gt by 2100. UNEP's guide helps Article 5 countries turn bank inventories into costed national refrigerant lifecycle plans. — [refindustry on UNEP guide](https://refindustry.com/news/unep-releases-guide-for-national-refrigerant-lifecycle-plans/)
- A 2025 Global Environmental Research paper notes that Kigali phases down upstream production/consumption but assumes the installed bank will eventually be emitted. It calls the lack of HFC destruction mechanisms in Article 5 contexts a crucial gap in carbon crediting. — [J-STAGE GER 29(2)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ger/29/2/29_29-2_08/_article/-char/ja)
- **Verra VM0016 v2.0** (active 12 Dec 2025, corrections 21 May 2026) is described by Verra as the first crediting-program methodology for destroying HFCs from specific sources. It applies to all Montreal Protocol parties including Article 5 and covers HCFC/HFC destruction in countries that have not finished phase-out, under conditions. A-Gas and Tradewater were external funders. v1.1 remains active until 31 Dec 2026. — [Verra release](https://verra.org/verra-releases-revised-methodology-for-the-destruction-of-ozone-depleting-substances-and-hydrofluorocarbons/); [VM0016 page](https://verra.org/methodologies/VM0016)
- **Isometric HFC & ODS Recovery and Destruction Protocol v1.0**, certified 14 May 2026, requires per-container weighing on a calibrated scale, compositional sampling (most conservative GWP result used), **chain-of-custody tracking from intake through destruction for every container**, and 99.99% DRE. Country eligibility lists are updated annually so destruction does not incentivise new production/import. Recoolit is the first supplier. — [Isometric](https://isometric.com/writing-articles/a-new-protocol-for-destroying-hydrofluorocarbons-and-ozone-depleting-substances)

CAR's Mexico Halocarbon Protocol also exists. — [ACR intl ODS](https://acrcarbon.org/methodology/destruction-of-ozone-depleting-substances-from-international-sources/); [ACR Canada update](https://acrcarbon.org/news/policy-update-methodologies-related-to-acr-ods-projects-in-canada/); [Yale primer](https://carboncontainmentlab.yale.edu/documents/fluorocarbon-methodologies-primer.pdf)

Critics showed perverse incentives (HCFC-22 output at a Ulsan plant rose from about 4,111 to about 7,000 t/yr after registration). The CDM EB suspended issuance for several projects. Giving Green found some claims hard to verify and asked for public financials. — [Giving Green](https://www.givinggreen.earth/research/tradewater); [ISC3](https://isc3.org/page/news/tradewater-reducing-the-worlds-carbon-footprint)

### Inferences
- The credit market for Article 5 HFC destruction is **brand new (2025–2026)** and multi-registry: Verra, Isometric, ACR and soon Taiwan's domestic scheme, possibly Article 6.4. New, fragmented, high-GWP-per-kg credit markets with an HFC-23 scandal history are exactly where a shared, tamper-evident chain-of-custody and anti-double-issuance layer is most justified. Isometric already *requires* per-container chain of custody, so there is demand for the data structure the idea proposes.
- The main residual integrity risks a ledger can address are: (1) virgin or stockpiled gas laundered as "recovered from equipment"; (2) the same container/kg claimed on two registries; (3) recovered kg counted toward a national phase-down or reclaim claim and also sold as an offset. Only (1) and (2) are supported by the critiques found. (3) is a reasoned risk without a documented case.

### Gaps
- **No documented case or formal analysis found of double-claiming between destruction credits and Kigali/national HFC phase-down accounting.** Do not present it as an established problem. Present it as a design safeguard. Kigali counts consumption (production + import − export, excluding destroyed amounts), so the interaction is technical and should be checked with the Ozone Secretariat's reporting rules.
- No issued-volume or price data for VM0016 v2.0 or Isometric HFC credits yet.

## Q9. Final verdict: need, distinctiveness, blockchain justification, framing, demo, objections

### Takeaway
**The broad idea, a full lifecycle ledger from importer to destruction, does not survive as stated.** It duplicates the EU F-gas Portal/CERTEX (import/quota), Türkiye's FARAVET/EKOMVET, Italy's Banca Dati, France's Trackdéchets BSFF and Japan's statutory RaMS. Its photo/lab AI duplicates Recoolit. **A narrower version survives.** It is a cross-organisation, cross-registry *mass-conservation and provenance* layer that makes two specific frauds structurally hard: (1) virgin/illegal HFC laundered as "reclaimed" or "recovered", and (2) the same destroyed kg credited twice or credited without an equipment-origin chain. Its AI flags mass-balance anomalies, both reported-vs-physical leakage and sales exceeding quota-backed inflows. This is blockchain-justified only where no single trusted authority spans all parties: cross-border flows such as Türkiye→EU, multi-registry Article 5 destruction credits, and competitor reclaimers. For a single EU member state, a central database is the honest answer.

### Cited Findings (evidence for the surviving gap)
- Siloed digital systems coexist with rampant fraud. Italy has the EU Portal plus the Banca Dati, yet has the largest seizures and alleged "nearly half illegal". The fraud modes listed by EIA (virgin as reclaimed, quota exceedance, false invoices) are cross-segment inconsistencies. — [refindustry/EIA](https://refindustry.com/news/eia-report-tracks-illegal-hfc-trade-into-eu-supply-chains/); [Banca Dati](https://www.fi.camcom.gov.it/sites/default/files/uploads/Registri_Albi_Certificazioni/Ambiente/Convegni_e_Seminari/Interventi_Fgas_FOCUS.pdf)
- "Reclaimed" status carries regulatory value: an EU servicing-ban exemption to 2030 and California's certified-reclaimed exemption. Industry itself flags traceability problems with recovered gas. — [Castel](https://castel.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024_NEW_F_GAS_Regulation_Castel.pdf); [CARB comment](https://www.arb.ca.gov/lists/com-attach/36-hfc2020-AHJRMlAyBToBZgNq.pdf)
- New multi-registry credit supply (Verra VM0016 v2.0 Dec 2025, Isometric May 2026, ACR, Taiwan draft July 2026) and Isometric's explicit per-container chain-of-custody requirement. — [Verra](https://verra.org/methodologies/VM0016); [Isometric](https://isometric.com/writing-articles/a-new-protocol-for-destroying-hydrofluorocarbons-and-ozone-depleting-substances); [PTS](https://news.pts.org.tw/article/818802)
- Japanese experts call for refrigerant credits plus integrated traceability data, and for exporting the model to SE Asia. — [IIR](https://iifiir.org/en/news/japan-explores-policy-and-digital-tools-for-a-refrigerant-circular-economy)

### Inferences: recommended framing

**Sharpest one-sentence problem statement (proposed):**
"Every country now digitises *pieces* of the HFC lifecycle (customs quotas, technician logbooks, waste slips), but no one checks that the kilograms add up across those pieces, so virgin smuggled gas is sold as 'reclaimed', leaks hide behind unreconciled top-ups, and the same tonne of destroyed super-pollutant can be credited twice."

Shorter variant: "HFC fraud lives in the gaps between registries. We make kilograms add up across them."

**Primary user (pick one for the demo):**
- Option A, recommended for the credit-integrity angle: a **destruction-credit verifier/registry and corporate credit buyer** for Article 5 projects (Indonesia/Türkiye/Taiwan). They need proof that each destroyed kg came from end-of-life equipment (not a virgin stockpile) and was not credited elsewhere.
- Option B, recommended for the Türkiye/COP31 angle: a **national ozone unit or customs/market-surveillance officer** reconciling FARAVET quota-backed inflows against distributor sales and EKOMVET charges. The secondary user is a **reclaimer** who wants a premium for provably-reclaimed gas.
- Avoid choosing "technicians" as the primary user. They bear the burden and get little value (see the EPA QR backlash).

**Minimal compelling demo (5 actors, 3 cylinders, ~10 minutes):**
1. An importer mints 100 kg R-404A against a (mock) FARAVET/F-gas Portal quota. The token is bound to signed QR/NFC cylinder IDs.
2. A distributor transfers 60 kg to technician cylinders. It then *tries to sell 80 kg* and the contract rejects it (conservation). The AI also flags a second distributor whose sales exceed its quota-backed purchases (simulated off-ledger invoice data).
3. A technician charges equipment and logs top-ups over 3 "years". The AI flags an asset whose cumulative top-ups exceed X% of nameplate charge (leak), echoing Japan's 575 t reported vs 6,283 t estimated gap.
4. At end of life, 8 kg is recovered into a recovery cylinder, weighed and sampled, and a signed lab certificate is attached.
5. A reclaimer tries to sell 20 kg as "reclaimed R-404A" with only 8 kg of documented recovered input. The contract caps reclaimed output at documented input minus losses, and the extra 12 kg is flagged as possible virgin laundering.
6. A cement kiln destroys 5 kg. A destruction token is minted only if the chain links to an equipment-origin recovery event and the lab analysis. An attempt to register the same cylinder event on a second (mock) registry is rejected via a shared nullifier/hash. A Sankey dashboard shows the kg mass balance per actor.

**Blockchain justification (honest version):** multiple competing firms, several jurisdictions (Türkiye→EU) and several credit registries must agree on one kg balance, and none trusts the others to host it. Public, append-only state lets buyers and registries check uniqueness without a central operator. Smart contracts make mass conservation a hard constraint rather than an after-the-fact audit. Do not claim blockchain verifies physical gas.

**Recommended cuts:** drop or minimise **post-quantum signatures** (no evidence of a threat model that needs them, and judges may read it as buzzword stacking). Keep **IoT leak prediction** as optional. Japan already promotes continuous leak monitoring, so it is not novel. Lead with **mass-balance anomaly detection**, the AI piece most tied to the ledger.

**3 hardest judge objections and honest answers:**
1. **"Garbage in, garbage out: the chain can't see what's inside a cylinder."** True. The ledger does not verify physics. It forces every claim to be *consistent across independent counterparties* (seller out = buyer in, reclaimed out ≤ recovered in, destroyed ≤ held), so a lie needs multi-party collusion. Physical anchors are weighing plus lab sampling at recovery and destruction, which Isometric already mandates, plus signed lab certificates. The AI targets the residual collusion by flagging statistically impossible balances.
2. **"Why blockchain? The EU F-gas Portal, Japan RaMS, Italy's Banca Dati and France's Trackdéchets already exist, and a regulator can run a database."** Partly concede: within one state with a strong regulator, yes. Then show Italy, which has both systems and is still the worst hotspot, because segments are not reconciled. The use case is the cross-border, cross-registry layer (Türkiye→EU, Verra vs Isometric vs national schemes) where no single authority has jurisdiction. Offer to *ingest* data from those national systems rather than replace them.
3. **"Destruction credits are greenwashing with a scandalous history (HFC-23); you're building more of it."** Acknowledge the HFC-23 perverse-incentive history and EIA's opposition. The design responds directly: credits only for kg with an equipment-origin chain (no stockpiled or virgin gas), a transparent per-kg audit trail, and cross-registry uniqueness. These target the "improper management and accounting" concern EIA raises. Position credits as one optional funding stream, since financing for banks is "inadequate" per EIA. The ledger is equally useful for regulatory reclaim certification without any offsets.
- Bonus objection, adoption burden: the US EPA QR mandate was vacated and removed after industry pushback. Answer: adoption is pull-based. Only actors who want credit eligibility or the reclaimed-gas premium need to participate, and data entry reuses existing national records.

**Verdict on need / distinctiveness / blockchain-fit:**
- Need: **high** (well-quantified emissions and fraud).
- Distinctiveness: **medium**. Concept-level prior art exists (CCCANZ 2023, Recoolit app, RaMS). The mass-conservation and cross-registry-uniqueness angle is not shown by any system found.
- Blockchain-fit: **medium-high for the cross-border/credit-integrity framing, low for a domestic EU/Japan logbook framing.**

### Gaps
- No interview or primary evidence from regulators (Türkiye ozone unit, Italian roundtable) that they want cross-segment reconciliation. Treat the user need as inferred.
- Reconciliation granularity (per cylinder vs per company per period) needs to be decided against real data fields in FARAVET/EKOMVET. Those fields were not confirmed.
- The double-claiming risk with Kigali accounting is a design safeguard, not a documented problem (see Q5).
